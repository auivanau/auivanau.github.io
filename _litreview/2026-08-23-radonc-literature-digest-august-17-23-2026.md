# Weekly Radiation Oncology Literature Surveillance Digest: August 17–23, 2026

## Bottom Line This Week

The most clinically meaningful paper this week is the prospective phase I **PROMISE 006** trial evaluating a thoracic “Quad Shot” regimen for symptomatic central lung tumours in patients with stage IV NSCLC receiving systemic therapy. Up to three cycles of 14.8 Gy in 4 twice-daily fractions over 2 days were feasible without disrupting systemic therapy, with encouraging patient-reported improvements in dyspnea, cough, and hemoptysis. This remains phase I evidence and should not displace established palliative thoracic schedules, but it is a credible strategy for selected patients in whom symptom relief and minimizing systemic-therapy interruption are priorities.

The other major clinically useful paper is a heavily reirradiated **proton spine hypofractionation** series. In 52 patients with 58 spinal lesions, most treated with 40 Gy in 5 fractions and 81% receiving reirradiation, 1-year local failure was 10% and 2-year local failure was 22%. The caution is equally important: all grade 3 toxicities occurred in patients receiving a third or fourth overlapping radiation course. This is useful evidence for highly selected reirradiation cases, not a general argument that protons make repeated spine irradiation “safe.”

The most immediately interesting supportive-care paper is a phase I/II trial of **lithium-containing mouthwash during head and neck radiotherapy**. Severe oral mucositis fell from 52.1% with placebo to 34.2% with lithium mouthwash, with a later onset of severe mucositis and a signal for improved taste dysfunction. The study is single-institutional and requires external replication, but the intervention is simple enough that a confirmatory multicentre trial could have substantial practical impact.

The most relevant proton/physics papers are a clinical series of **proton hemithoracic pleural irradiation** and a retrospective carbon-ion reirradiation analysis linking LET distribution with local control in recurrent nasopharyngeal cancer. Both are technically interesting and clinically plausible, but neither provides randomized evidence that particle therapy improves outcomes over high-quality photon therapy.

The MRI-based internal mammary node paper is provocative because it suggests that response to neoadjuvant therapy may help select which initially involved internal mammary nodes require a postoperative boost. However, it is retrospective and vulnerable to treatment-selection bias, so omission of boost based on MRI complete response should remain investigational.

Overall, this was a **moderately useful but not practice-changing week**. The strongest signal is implementation-focused: how to deliver palliation efficiently, how far reirradiation can be pushed in selected patients, and how imaging or particle-therapy biology might refine treatment. There was no high-level randomized trial establishing a new standard of care, and no AI paper this week met the threshold for inclusion over stronger clinical evidence.

## Must Read

### 1. A Phase I Study of Cyclical Hypofractionated Palliative Radiotherapy (Quad Shot) for Central Lung Tumors in Stage IV NSCLC

Citation: Zhang YH, Iqbal A, Gelblum D, Ma J, Yorke E, Yang JT, Gillespie EF, Toumbacaris N, Zhang Z, Brooks P, Khalyat E, Shaverdian N, Brennan V, Guttmann DM, Imber BS, Iyengar P, Shin J, Simone CB II, Wu AJ, Rimner A, Gomez D, Shepherd A. A Phase I Study of Cyclical Hypofractionated Palliative Radiotherapy (Quad Shot) for Central Lung Tumors in Patients with Stage IV Non-Small Cell Lung Cancer (NSCLC): Results from the PROMISE 006 Trial. International Journal of Radiation Oncology, Biology, Physics. Published online August 18, 2026.[^1]

Journal/source: International Journal of Radiation Oncology, Biology, Physics  
Publication type: Peer-reviewed prospective phase I dose-escalation trial  
One-line takeaway: Up to three Quad Shot cycles were feasible alongside systemic therapy, with limited high-grade toxicity and encouraging symptom improvement in advanced NSCLC.

Evidence tier: Prospective phase I evidence; clinically relevant but not comparative or practice-defining.

Importance score: 8/10

Key limitations:

- Phase I design with a small sample size and no randomized comparator.
- Symptom responses were patient-reported and not compared with standard palliative thoracic schedules such as 8 Gy ×1, 20 Gy in 5 fractions, or 30 Gy in 10 fractions.
- The twice-daily regimen requires same-day treatment logistics and adequate interfraction spacing.
- Results may not generalize to patients with very poor performance status, major airway compromise, or substantially different systemic-therapy regimens.

Practical radiation oncology relevance:
This is a plausible niche option for symptomatic thoracic disease when clinicians want rapid palliation without substantially interrupting immunotherapy or chemotherapy. Each cycle delivered 14.8 Gy in 4 twice-daily fractions over 2 days, and three cycles were established as the maximum tolerated schedule. The key next question is whether the approach provides better symptom durability, convenience, or systemic-therapy continuity than simpler established palliative schedules.

### 2. Local Control and Outcomes Associated With Proton Spine Hypofractionated Radiation Therapy

Citation: Jackson CB, Higginson DS, Lin H, Mah D, Haseltine J, Mueller BA, Schmitt AM, Vaynrub M, Newman WC, Bou-Nassif R, Lis E, Barzilai O, Bilsky MH, Yamada Y. Local control and outcomes associated with proton spine hypofractionated radiation therapy. International Journal of Radiation Oncology, Biology, Physics. Published online August 19, 2026.[^2]

Journal/source: International Journal of Radiation Oncology, Biology, Physics  
Publication type: Peer-reviewed retrospective clinical outcomes study  
One-line takeaway: Proton hypofractionated radiotherapy achieved high local control in a heavily reirradiated spine-metastasis cohort, but serious toxicity clustered in patients receiving third or fourth overlapping radiation courses.

Evidence tier: Retrospective single-institution cohort with longitudinal MRI follow-up.

Importance score: 7.5/10

Key limitations:

- Retrospective treatment selection strongly favors patients considered suitable for aggressive reirradiation.
- Only 52 patients and 58 lesions were included.
- Proton therapy was not compared directly with photon SBRT or other salvage approaches.
- Cumulative EQD2 does not fully capture spatial dose overlap, variable fractionation, spinal cord recovery, or uncertainties in prior dose reconstruction.

Practical radiation oncology relevance:
This is useful for difficult spine reirradiation discussions. Most lesions received 40 Gy in 5 fractions, the median cumulative EQD2 was 115 Gy, and 81% of treatments were reirradiation. The 1-year local-failure rate of 10% is encouraging, but the five grade 3 events—including myelitis, esophageal fistula, esophageal stricture, wound dehiscence, and bowel necrosis—underscore that repeated overlapping treatment remains high risk even with protons.

## Worth Skimming

### 3. Effect of Lithium Mouthwash on Oral Mucositis and Taste Dysfunction in Head and Neck Radiotherapy

Citation: Li Z, Pei Y, Zhang Z, et al. Effect of Lithium Mouthwash on Oral Mucositis and Taste Dysfunction in Head and Neck Radiotherapy: The Phase I/II Clinical Trial. International Journal of Radiation Oncology, Biology, Physics. Published online August 21, 2026.[^3]

Journal/source: International Journal of Radiation Oncology, Biology, Physics  
Publication type: Peer-reviewed prospective phase I/II clinical trial  
One-line takeaway: Lithium-containing mouthwash reduced severe oral mucositis and delayed its onset during head and neck radiotherapy, with a concurrent signal for improved taste dysfunction.

Evidence tier: Prospective placebo-controlled phase II evidence after phase I dose finding.

Importance score: 7/10

Key limitations:

- Single-institution study requiring external validation.
- The phase II cohort was modest in size and not powered for uncommon safety events.
- Details of concurrent systemic therapy, oral-cavity dose, baseline dental status, and supportive-care practices may influence generalizability.
- A statistically significant reduction in mucositis does not automatically establish meaningful downstream benefits in feeding-tube use, treatment interruption, weight loss, hospitalization, or oncologic outcomes.

Practical radiation oncology relevance:
Severe oral mucositis remains a major problem during head and neck radiotherapy. In the phase II portion, severe mucositis occurred in 34.2% of patients receiving lithium mouthwash versus 52.1% with placebo. If reproduced in a multicentre study with patient-centred endpoints, this could become a low-complexity supportive-care intervention with real day-to-day value.

### 4. MRI-Based Internal Mammary Node Response After Neoadjuvant Therapy to Guide Postoperative Internal Mammary Node Boost Radiotherapy in Breast Cancer

Citation: Peng Y, Xiang Y, Tie J, Zhang S, Shi C, Guo C, Wang W. MRI-Based Internal Mammary Node Response After Neoadjuvant Therapy to Guide Postoperative Internal Mammary Node Radiotherapy Boost in Breast Cancer. International Journal of Radiation Oncology, Biology, Physics. Published online August 21, 2026.[^4]

Journal/source: International Journal of Radiation Oncology, Biology, Physics  
Publication type: Peer-reviewed retrospective clinical investigation  
One-line takeaway: Among patients with initially imaging-positive internal mammary nodes, MRI response after neoadjuvant therapy may identify patients who derive less benefit from an additional postoperative nodal boost.

Evidence tier: Large retrospective single-centre cohort with subgroup and interaction analyses.

Importance score: 6.5/10

Key limitations:

- Retrospective, nonrandomized selection for boost introduces substantial confounding by indication.
- Internal mammary nodal involvement and response were imaging-defined rather than pathologically confirmed.
- The key predictive claim depends on interaction and subgroup analyses with relatively limited event numbers.
- Results require prospective validation before using MRI complete response to omit boost routinely.

Practical radiation oncology relevance:
This paper addresses a real uncertainty in breast radiotherapy: what to do with an internal mammary node that was radiographically involved before neoadjuvant therapy but disappears afterward. The study suggests residual disease may identify patients more likely to benefit from escalation, while complete MRI response may support de-escalation. For now, the finding is hypothesis-generating rather than a new standard.

### 5. Proton Therapy for Hemithoracic Pleural Irradiation: A Step Toward Safer Radiation Treatment

Citation: Panse D, Simone CB II, Shepherd A, Ohri N, Shaverdian N, Fox JL, Lin H, Yang S, Kang M, Han Z, Shim A, Rosenzweig K, Samstein RM, Lazarev S. Proton Therapy for Hemithoracic Pleural Irradiation: A Step Toward Safer Radiation Treatment. International Journal of Radiation Oncology, Biology, Physics. Published online August 20, 2026.[^5]

Journal/source: International Journal of Radiation Oncology, Biology, Physics  
Publication type: Peer-reviewed retrospective clinical investigation  
One-line takeaway: Intensity-modulated proton therapy enabled hemithoracic pleural irradiation with very low contralateral lung exposure and acceptable early toxicity in a small heterogeneous cohort.

Evidence tier: Retrospective single-institution proton therapy series.

Importance score: 6.5/10

Key limitations:

- Small cohort with heterogeneous diseases including mesothelioma, thymic malignancies, and sarcoma.
- No contemporaneous photon comparator.
- Median follow-up was short for late pulmonary, cardiac, and second-malignancy outcomes.
- Selection for proton therapy and treatment at a high-volume centre limits generalizability.

Practical radiation oncology relevance:
Hemithoracic pleural irradiation is one of the clearest anatomical scenarios in which protons may offer a meaningful dosimetric advantage. The reported contralateral lung doses were extremely low while maintaining target coverage. The study supports feasibility, but the clinical value of that dosimetric advantage still requires longer follow-up and ideally comparative evidence.

## Save for Later

### 6. Association Between LET Distribution and Local Recurrence After Carbon-Ion Reirradiation for Recurrent Nasopharyngeal Carcinoma

Citation: Du J, Huang Q, Wang W, Sheng Y, Hu J, Kong L. Association Between Linear Energy Transfer Distribution and Local Recurrence in Recurrent Nasopharyngeal Carcinoma Reirradiated With Intensity-Modulated Carbon Ion Therapy. International Journal of Radiation Oncology, Biology, Physics. Published online August 18, 2026.[^6]

Journal/source: International Journal of Radiation Oncology, Biology, Physics  
Publication type: Peer-reviewed retrospective particle-therapy and radiobiology study  
One-line takeaway: Higher minimum tumour LET was associated with better local control after carbon-ion reirradiation, generating potential targets for LET-guided optimization.

Evidence tier: Retrospective single-centre hypothesis-generating analysis.

Importance score: 5.5/10

Key limitations:

- Only 76 patients were included.
- LET metrics were analyzed retrospectively and are correlated with tumour size, beam arrangement, and dose distribution.
- The proposed LET threshold may be dataset-specific and requires external validation.
- Carbon-ion therapy has extremely limited availability, reducing generalizability.

Practical radiation oncology relevance:
This paper is conceptually important for particle therapy because it suggests that physical dose alone may not fully describe treatment quality in carbon-ion reirradiation. The association between higher GTV LET and improved local control is interesting, but the analysis is not sufficient to support routine LET-based prescription thresholds without prospective validation.

## Low Priority

### 7. Implementation of a Radiation Oncology Incident Learning System in a Limited-Resource Setting

Citation: Colbert C, et al. Implementation of a radiation oncology incident learning system in a limited resource setting: risk analysis and safety culture. International Journal of Radiation Oncology, Biology, Physics. Published online August 21, 2026.[^7]

Journal/source: International Journal of Radiation Oncology, Biology, Physics  
Publication type: Peer-reviewed quality-improvement and safety-culture implementation study  
One-line takeaway: A structured incident-learning approach can support risk identification and safety culture in a resource-constrained radiation oncology environment.

Evidence tier: Operational quality-improvement evidence; not a patient-outcome study.

Importance score: 5/10

Key limitations:

- Safety-culture and reporting metrics are surrogate process outcomes.
- Results are highly dependent on local staffing, leadership, reporting culture, and infrastructure.
- Increased reporting can reflect better safety culture rather than more or fewer actual errors.
- Findings do not establish a direct reduction in patient harm.

Practical radiation oncology relevance:
Incident learning is a core component of safe radiotherapy, especially as workflows become increasingly automated and adaptive. This paper is more relevant to department leadership, quality committees, and medical physics than to day-to-day prescribing. Its value is implementation-oriented rather than clinical.
