*Monthly Oncology Highlights* is a deliberately selective record of developments likely to retain clinical relevance after the immediate news cycle. For August 2026, inclusion is based on the meaningful public release date—particularly online-ahead-of-print publication—rather than a later print-issue date.

## Major Developments

### ASTRO updates pancreatic radiotherapy guidance across the disease continuum

ASTRO published an updated clinical practice guideline for pancreatic cancer on August 11. The guideline conditionally recommends radiotherapy in selected preoperative and postoperative settings for resectable disease, recommends preoperative radiotherapy for borderline-resectable disease and definitive radiotherapy for locally advanced disease, and supports conventional or moderately hypofractionated approaches depending on setting. For definitive treatment, it also incorporates emerging dose-escalation strategies using SBRT or moderately hypofractionated radiotherapy and recommends adaptive radiotherapy when dose-escalated SBRT is used.

The update also addresses recurrence, reirradiation, palliation, and oligometastatic or oligoprogressive disease, including conditional recommendations for metastasis-directed radiotherapy in selected patients. The significance is less a single new standard than a broader, more explicit framework for where modern radiotherapy—including SBRT, adaptation, and reirradiation—can fit into multidisciplinary pancreatic cancer care.

**Source:** Chuong MD, Jethwa KR, Ludmir E, et al. *Practical Radiation Oncology*. Published online August 11, 2026. [doi:10.1016/j.prro.2026.07.002](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.prro.2026.07.002).

### FDA approves daraxonrasib, a RAS inhibitor, for metastatic pancreatic adenocarcinoma

On August 26, the FDA approved daraxonrasib for adults with metastatic pancreatic adenocarcinoma after at least one prior systemic therapy, or for patients who are not candidates for multiagent systemic therapy. In the randomized RASolute 302 trial of 500 patients whose disease had progressed after one prior line, daraxonrasib improved median overall survival to 13.2 months versus 6.7 months with physician's-choice chemotherapy (HR 0.40, 95% CI 0.30–0.53), with median progression-free survival of 7.2 versus 3.6 months (HR 0.49, 95% CI 0.38–0.64). Objective response rates were 30% and 11%, respectively.

This is a major systemic-therapy development in a disease where actionable treatment options have historically been limited, and it arrives in the same month as a substantial update to pancreatic radiotherapy guidance. The August event is specifically a U.S. approval: the FDA noted that the application was being reviewed concurrently with other regulators, including Health Canada, under Project Orbis.

**Source:** U.S. Food and Drug Administration. FDA approval notice, August 26, 2026. [FDA: Daraxonrasib for metastatic pancreatic adenocarcinoma](https://www.fda.gov/drugs/resources-information-approved-drugs/fda-approves-daraxonrasib-metastatic-pancreatic-adenocarcinoma).

### Phase III SHARE supports a shorter salvage radiotherapy course after prostatectomy, but does not establish superiority

The phase III SHARE trial randomized 316 patients with biochemical recurrence after radical prostatectomy to hypofractionated salvage radiotherapy (65 Gy in 26 fractions) or conventional fractionation (66 Gy in 33 fractions). At a median follow-up of 52.6 months, four-year biochemical progression-free survival was 80.1% with hypofractionation and 78.1% with conventional treatment. Distant metastasis-free survival and cancer-specific survival were also similar, while patient-reported quality of life was comparable between groups.

The trial did not meet its prespecified superiority endpoint, so the result should not be interpreted as formal proof of noninferiority. Grade 2 or higher gastrointestinal toxicity at four years was higher with hypofractionation (7.9% versus 0.7%), predominantly among patients treated without an endorectal balloon, although reported events had resolved by last follow-up. The study nevertheless provides randomized evidence supporting a seven-fraction-shorter salvage option and helps define the trade-offs that accompany it.

**Source:** *Salvage Hypofractionated Accelerated Versus Standard Radiotherapy for Biochemical Recurrence After Radical Prostatectomy: A Phase III Randomized Clinical Trial.* *Journal of Clinical Oncology*. Published online August 25, 2026. [doi:10.1200/JCO-25-02234](https://doi.org/10.1200/JCO-25-02234).

### FDA approves zanidatamab-based first-line therapy for HER2-positive advanced gastroesophageal adenocarcinoma

On August 25, the FDA approved zanidatamab-hrii with fluoropyrimidine/platinum chemotherapy and tislelizumab-jsgr for HER2-positive unresectable locally advanced or metastatic gastric, gastroesophageal-junction, or esophageal adenocarcinoma, and also approved zanidatamab with chemotherapy for HER2 IHC 3+ disease. In HERIZON-GEA-01, the triplet strategy produced a median overall survival of 26.4 months versus 19.2 months with trastuzumab plus chemotherapy (HR 0.72, 95% CI 0.57–0.90) and median progression-free survival of 12.4 versus 8.1 months (HR 0.63, 95% CI 0.51–0.78).

The pivotal trial had already been published earlier in 2026; the August development was the regulatory transition of those results into a new U.S. first-line option. For multidisciplinary oncology, the approval changes the U.S. first-line systemic options for the same unresectable or metastatic population frequently managed across medical, surgical, and radiation oncology, while the August event itself is regulatory rather than a new trial publication.

**Source:** U.S. Food and Drug Administration. FDA approval notice, August 25, 2026. [FDA: Zanidatamab-hrii and tislelizumab-jsgr for HER2-positive gastroesophageal adenocarcinoma](https://www.fda.gov/drugs/resources-information-approved-drugs/fda-approves-zanidatamab-hrii-and-tislelizumab-jsgr-her2-positive-gastric-gastroesophageal-junction). Primary HERIZON-GEA-01 report: *New England Journal of Medicine*. [doi:10.1056/NEJMoa2517729](https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJMoa2517729).

## Radiation Oncology & Technology

### Multimodal AI proposes risk-adapted surveillance after treatment for stage II nasopharyngeal carcinoma

A multicentre study of 2,148 patients with stage II nasopharyngeal carcinoma developed a transformer-based model integrating MRI and radiotherapy dose maps to estimate failure timing and generate individualized surveillance schedules. Reported discrimination was high in both internal and external validation cohorts, and the model-based strategy substantially reduced the number of scheduled follow-up visits for patients who remained recurrence-free while retaining high modeled sensitivity for failures.

The work is notable because it moves beyond image-only prediction toward using treatment-delivery information to guide survivorship care. It remains an emerging rather than practice-ready application: the surveillance strategy was evaluated using target-trial emulation rather than a prospective surveillance trial and requires prospective validation before replacing standard follow-up schedules.

**Source:** Zhang LN, Wang YT, Lan XW, et al. *International Journal of Radiation Oncology, Biology, Physics*. Published online August 18, 2026. [doi:10.1016/j.ijrobp.2026.08.020](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ijrobp.2026.08.020).

## Broader Oncology & Clinical Medicine

### FDA grants accelerated approval to oncolytic viral therapy plus nivolumab for PD-1-refractory melanoma

On August 6, the FDA granted accelerated approval to vusolimogene oderparepvec-wtpg combined with nivolumab for adults with unresectable advanced cutaneous melanoma that has progressed on a PD-1-blocking antibody regimen. In the single-arm IGNYTE study, 91 efficacy-evaluable patients with at least one noninjected measurable lesion had an objective response rate of 24.2% (95% CI 15.8–34.3), with a median duration of response of 14.1 months.

The approval adds an intratumoral oncolytic viral strategy to the post-PD-1 treatment landscape, but the evidentiary status is important: approval was based on response rate and durability rather than randomized survival data. Continued approval is contingent on confirmatory evidence, with the randomized phase III IGNYTE-3 trial evaluating overall survival.

**Source:** U.S. Food and Drug Administration. Accelerated approval notice, August 6, 2026. [FDA: Vusolimogene oderparepvec-wtpg with nivolumab for melanoma](https://www.fda.gov/drugs/resources-information-approved-drugs/fda-grants-accelerated-approval-vusolimogene-oderparepvec-wtpg-combination-nivolumab-melanoma).

## Worth Watching

### Intracranial B7-H3 CAR-T therapy in recurrent glioblastoma

A first-in-human phase I study treated 15 patients with recurrent glioblastoma using repeated intracranial B7-H3 CAR-T-cell infusions. No dose-limiting toxicity was observed, and the reported median overall survival from first infusion was 19.1 months, but the cohort was small and uncontrolled, making efficacy conclusions preliminary.

**Source:** Zhang Y, Chi X, Feng R, et al. *Nature Medicine*. Published August 6, 2026. [doi:10.1038/s41591-026-04557-6](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41591-026-04557-6).

### Antibody-drug conjugate plus PD-1 blockade shows a strong early signal in recurrent or metastatic nasopharyngeal carcinoma

In the phase I/II Magic-C001 study, 31 patients with recurrent or metastatic nasopharyngeal carcinoma previously treated with platinum chemotherapy and PD-1/PD-L1 blockade received becotatug vedotin plus pucotenlimab at the recommended phase II dose. The confirmed objective response rate was 71.0%, with a median duration of response of 14.0 months and median progression-free survival of 12.0 months; randomized phase III confirmation is ongoing.

**Source:** Ruan DY, Wang FH, Zhou Y, et al. *Journal of Clinical Oncology*. Published online August 25, 2026. [doi:10.1200/JCO-26-00640](https://doi.org/10.1200/JCO-26-00640).

### Proton FLASH shows ocular normal-tissue sparing in a preclinical model

A mouse study comparing conventional-dose-rate and proton FLASH irradiation reported substantially less late ocular injury after FLASH at 24 Gy while preserving comparable short-term antitumor effects in an intraocular melanoma model. The result adds to the biological rationale for FLASH but remains preclinical and does not establish a clinically usable therapeutic window.

**Source:** Amit U, Bell BA, El Hoyek E, et al. *International Journal of Radiation Oncology, Biology, Physics*. Published online August 26, 2026. [doi:10.1016/j.ijrobp.2026.08.045](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ijrobp.2026.08.045).

## August in Brief

August 2026 combined several developments with immediate clinical relevance and a smaller set of technologies still moving toward validation. Pancreatic cancer was unusually prominent, with both an updated radiotherapy guideline and a new RAS-directed systemic option; salvage prostate radiotherapy gained randomized evidence for a shorter schedule; and new FDA approvals changed systemic options in HER2-positive gastroesophageal cancer and PD-1-refractory melanoma. In radiation oncology research, individualized surveillance, intracranial cellular therapy, and FLASH continued to advance, but the most notable signals remain prospective-validation questions rather than established changes in routine practice.
