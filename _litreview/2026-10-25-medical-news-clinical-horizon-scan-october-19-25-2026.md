# Weekly Medical News and Clinical Horizon Scan: October 19–25, 2026

## Bottom Line This Week

**Most clinically important:** On October 19, the US FDA granted **accelerated approval to dostarlimab** for previously untreated stage II–III dMMR/MSI-H rectal cancer, a small but crucial molecular subgroup in which a nonoperative, organ-preserving strategy may avoid chemotherapy, radiotherapy, and surgery. The evidence remains single-arm and the durability of organ preservation requires longer follow-up. Health Canada has **accepted the rectal-cancer indication for review**, not approved it.

**Most important new therapy:** On October 20, the FDA approved **bepirovirsen**, the first antisense oligonucleotide for selected adults with chronic hepatitis B receiving suppressive antiviral treatment. In the pivotal program, 24% met criteria to stop nucleos(t)ide therapy and 19% maintained virologic control with undetectable HBsAg 24 weeks after stopping all therapy. This is a major functional-cure signal, but not a cure for most recipients; serious hepatic and hematologic toxicity warrants boxed warnings. The Canadian application has received priority review, but authorization was not verified.

**Most important randomized evidence:** Final phase III **sibeprenlimab** results showed near-stabilization of kidney function over 24 months in IgA nephropathy: annualized eGFR change **+0.3 versus −4.2 mL/min/1.73 m²/year** with placebo. A separate phase III atacicept analysis also showed a large eGFR benefit. These are substantially more persuasive than proteinuria-only readouts and make disease-modifying IgA nephropathy therapy one of the week's strongest signals. In oncology, **tarlatamab plus durvalumab maintenance** improved median overall survival from **12.7 to 21.1 months** in extensive-stage small-cell lung cancer in a prespecified interim analysis, although it adds cytokine-release syndrome risk.

**Most important safety/public-health update:** WHO's October 23 update reported **9,704 confirmed Bundibugyo Ebola cases and 4,687 deaths** in the Democratic Republic of the Congo, with an imported case in Nigeria and a contained cluster in Kenya. In Canada, a **Health Canada Class II recall of certain Abbott i-STAT high-sensitivity troponin I cartridges** warns of potentially falsely elevated results due to analytical interference. The latter is immediately relevant to emergency and cardiac care.

**Most credible emerging technology:** Personalized neoantigen mRNA therapy **intismeran plus pembrolizumab** reported improved two-year recurrence-free survival after resected high-risk melanoma (**82.2% versus 70.0%**), but the full phase III manuscript was not yet publicly verifiable. Circular RNA delivery of aquaporin-1 for radiation-induced xerostomia is a plausible restorative strategy, supported so far by only six patients.

**Hype check:** A much-discussed AI diagnostic-orchestration system scored approximately 83% on retrospective, unusually difficult written cases versus 36% for physicians with references. This is interesting technical research, **not** evidence of superior real-world diagnostic care or safe autonomous practice.

**Overall assessment:** **A high-importance week**, with two consequential FDA decisions, several substantial randomized clinical signals, and a Canadian device safety action. Some developments are immediately actionable in the United States or in specialized public-health practice; others require Canadian authorization, reimbursement, longer clinical follow-up, or prospective patient-outcome validation. For generalists, the biggest near-term implications are accurate dMMR testing and referral, HBV specialist awareness, recognizing false-positive troponin risk, and Ebola travel/exposure assessment.

## Must Know

### 1. Dostarlimab Receives US Accelerated Approval for Organ Preservation in dMMR Rectal Cancer

Citation: US Food and Drug Administration. FDA Approves Immunotherapy That May Allow Certain Patients with Rectal Cancer to Avoid Chemotherapy, Radiation and Surgery.[^1]

Journal/source: FDA; AZUR-1 and Memorial Sloan Kettering clinical programs

Publication type: Accelerated regulatory approval based on nonrandomized phase II clinical evidence

One-line takeaway: Single-agent dostarlimab can produce durable clinical complete responses in selected previously untreated stage II–III dMMR/MSI-H rectal cancers, potentially avoiding chemoradiation and resection.

Evidence tier: Compelling multicentre single-arm clinical response evidence; no randomized comparison or mature long-term oncologic equivalence.

Evidence maturity: Ready for practice in the US under a narrow accelerated indication; confirmatory evidence still required.

Practice impact: Now for eligible US patients in expert multidisciplinary programs; possibly soon in Canada.

Canadian relevance:
Dostarlimab is authorized in Canada for selected endometrial-cancer indications, **not** this rectal-cancer indication. Health Canada accepted the supplemental submission for dMMR/MSI-H stage II–III rectal cancer under the NOC/c and Project Orbis pathways in September 2026; Canadian approval and reimbursement for rectal cancer were not verified as of October 25.

Importance score: 9.5/10

Key limitations:

* The 154-patient AZUR-1 trial is single-arm; nearly three-quarters avoided surgery, radiotherapy, or chemotherapy after median follow-up exceeding two years, but there is no randomized comparison with total neoadjuvant therapy.
* Clinical complete response is not synonymous with pathologic cure; local regrowth and distant recurrence require prolonged surveillance and prompt salvage pathways.
* The eligible molecular subgroup is small (approximately 3% by the FDA estimate), and immune-related adverse events can be severe or fatal.

Practical medical relevance:
Every newly diagnosed locally advanced rectal cancer should have reliable mismatch-repair or microsatellite-instability testing. Patients with dMMR/MSI-H disease should be discussed in a multidisciplinary colorectal program capable of endoscopic/MRI response assessment, watch-and-wait surveillance, and salvage surgery. This is not a blanket recommendation to omit standard treatment for mismatch-repair-proficient disease.

### 2. Bepirovirsen: First US-Approved Antisense Therapy Offers a Chance of Functional Hepatitis B Cure

Citation: US Food and Drug Administration. FDA Approves First-of-Its-Kind Chronic Hepatitis B Treatment.[^2]

Journal/source: FDA; phase III B-Well development program

Publication type: Regulatory approval supported by randomized placebo-controlled trials

One-line takeaway: For a selected, virologically suppressed noncirrhotic HBV population, adding bepirovirsen allows a minority to stop all chronic HBV therapy while maintaining HBsAg loss and viral control.

Evidence tier: Randomized pivotal-trial evidence with clinically meaningful treatment-discontinuation endpoints; safety monitoring essential.

Evidence maturity: Ready for tightly selected US practice.

Practice impact: Now in the US; possibly soon in Canada if review succeeds.

Canadian relevance:
GSK announced an April 2026 Canadian new-drug submission with **Health Canada priority review**. Canadian market authorization and public reimbursement were not verified by October 25. The FDA approval and companion quantitative HBsAg test do not automatically authorize either in Canada.

Importance score: 9.3/10

Key limitations:

* Only 24% of treated patients met protocol criteria to stop antivirals and 19% maintained undetectable HBsAg with viral control 24 weeks after all treatment stopped; most patients did not achieve this outcome.
* A boxed warning covers serious hepatic reactions and thrombocytopenia, with additional renal, inflammatory, and administration-related warnings.
* Evidence applies to noncirrhotic adults already on antivirals for at least six months with suppressed HBV DNA and HBsAg ≤3,000 IU/mL; long-term relapse, hepatocellular-carcinoma risk, and real-world monitoring burden remain uncertain.

Practical medical relevance:
This is the first approved HBV-directed antisense treatment to offer a realistic finite-therapy pathway for some patients. It should be prescribed and monitored by HBV-experienced clinicians with serial liver enzymes, platelets, virology, and explicit stopping/restarting criteria. “Functional cure” means sustained HBsAg loss and viral control, not proven eradication of all viral reservoirs.

### 3. Sibeprenlimab Preserves Kidney Function in Final Phase III IgA Nephropathy Analysis

Citation: Rizk DV, et al., for the VISIONARY Trial Investigators. Sibeprenlimab in IgA Nephropathy — Final Phase 3 Trial Analysis.[^3]

Journal/source: New England Journal of Medicine; ASN Kidney Week 2026

Publication type: Final phase III double-blind randomized placebo-controlled trial analysis

One-line takeaway: APRIL inhibition with sibeprenlimab stabilized eGFR over 24 months compared with substantial decline on placebo in IgA nephropathy.

Evidence tier: High: 510-patient randomized trial with two-year eGFR slope and a prespecified kidney-disease progression composite.

Evidence maturity: Potentially practice-changing; regulatory implementation pending by jurisdiction.

Practice impact: Possibly soon.

Canadian relevance:
No Health Canada authorization or provincial reimbursement for sibeprenlimab in IgA nephropathy could be verified by October 25. Canada already has other IgAN-directed options, including Health Canada–approved atrasentan for proteinuria reduction, but comparative sequencing and funding pathways are unsettled.

Importance score: 9.2/10

Key limitations:

* The 24-month eGFR slope (+0.3 vs −4.2 mL/min/1.73 m²/year) is persuasive, but lifelong kidney-failure prevention and very long-term safety remain unknown.
* The kidney composite was uncommon (0.4% vs 11.2%) and its striking relative effect should not be overinterpreted as a precisely estimated effect on dialysis or transplantation.
* Background supportive care and evolving IgAN therapeutics complicate indirect comparisons; immunologic effects require infection and immunoglobulin surveillance.

Practical medical relevance:
The eGFR-slope difference was 4.5 mL/min/1.73 m²/year (95% CI 3.6–5.4), and the 24-month between-group eGFR difference was 9.2 mL/min/1.73 m². These are far more clinically interpretable than proteinuria alone and support earlier disease-modifying referral for patients with progressive IgAN despite optimized supportive care.

## Important Developments

### 4. Tarlatamab–Durvalumab Maintenance Produces a Major Survival Gain in Extensive-Stage SCLC

Citation: Pérol M, Yoshida T, Ahn M-J, et al. Tarlatamab–Durvalumab Maintenance for Extensive-Stage Small-Cell Lung Cancer.[^4]

Journal/source: New England Journal of Medicine; ESMO 2026

Publication type: Prespecified interim analysis of an international randomized phase III trial

One-line takeaway: DLL3-directed T-cell engagement added to durvalumab maintenance prolonged median overall survival from 12.7 to 21.1 months after initial chemoimmunotherapy.

Evidence tier: High: 563-patient randomized trial with overall survival as a primary endpoint.

Evidence maturity: Potentially practice-changing.

Practice impact: Possibly soon; not automatically a licensed first-line maintenance regimen.

Canadian relevance:
Tarlatamab (Imdelltra) has a Canadian authorization for **previously treated** extensive-stage SCLC; that is not an authorization for first-line maintenance with durvalumab. Canadian approval and public funding for this new combination were not verified.

Importance score: 9.1/10

Key limitations:

* The HR for death was 0.42 (95% CI 0.32–0.54), but the analysis was interim with approximately 16 months median follow-up.
* Open-label trial; grade ≥3 adverse events occurred in 46.8% versus 40.6%, and cytokine-release syndrome occurred in nearly half of combination recipients.
* Requires expertise in bispecific-agent initiation and monitoring; eligibility is restricted to patients without progression after induction chemoimmunotherapy.

Practical medical relevance:
The absolute median overall-survival gain of 8.4 months is substantial in extensive-stage SCLC, where historical survival is short. This may move DLL3-targeted immunotherapy earlier, but Canadian programs must plan for CRS monitoring, toxicity management, and funding rather than treating a publication as immediate approval.

### 5. Retatrutide Improves Moderate-to-Severe Obstructive Sleep Apnea in Two Phase III Trials

Citation: Grunstein RR, Somers VK, Azarbarzin A, et al. Retatrutide Once Weekly for Treatment of Obstructive Sleep Apnea.[^5]

Journal/source: New England Journal of Medicine; European Sleep Research Society Congress

Publication type: Two double-blind randomized phase III placebo-controlled trials

One-line takeaway: Investigational triple GIP/GLP-1/glucagon agonism substantially lowered apnea–hypopnea index (AHI) over 80 weeks in adults with overweight/obesity and OSA, with or without diabetes.

Evidence tier: High for AHI reduction: 691 randomized participants across two trials; less mature for hard sleep/cardiovascular outcomes.

Evidence maturity: Potentially practice-changing after regulatory review; not ready for routine prescription.

Practice impact: Possibly soon.

Canadian relevance:
Retatrutide is **not Health Canada approved**, nor is it an approved OSA therapy in the US. Canadian clinical-trial access is not commercial availability or reimbursement. Existing OSA care, including PAP therapy and individualized weight management, remains standard.

Importance score: 8.7/10

Key limitations:

* In participants without diabetes, the 9-mg arm reduced AHI by 34.3 events/hour versus 9.9 with placebo; with diabetes, the 9-mg arm reduced AHI by 24.7 versus 10.3. These are physiological endpoints, not proven reductions in cardiovascular events or mortality.
* No direct head-to-head comparison with PAP therapy; weight loss likely mediates much of the observed OSA improvement.
* Gastrointestinal adverse effects were common; long-term tolerability, access, and cost remain unresolved.

Practical medical relevance:
This supports treating obesity as a major modifiable contributor to OSA rather than considering OSA solely a device-management problem. It does not justify discontinuing CPAP without reassessment. Clinicians should be prepared for patients asking about retatrutide despite its investigational status.

### 6. Anbenitamab Plus Albumin-Bound Docetaxel Improves PFS in HER2-Positive Advanced Breast Cancer

Citation: Wang J, et al., for the HEALER investigators. Anbenitamab plus HB1801 for HER2-Positive Advanced Breast Cancer.[^6]

Journal/source: New England Journal of Medicine; ESMO 2026

Publication type: Randomized phase III active-comparator trial

One-line takeaway: A HER2-targeted bispecific antibody plus albumin-bound docetaxel improved 24-month progression-free survival to 58.5% versus 34.4% with standard trastuzumab/pertuzumab/docetaxel.

Evidence tier: High for progression-free survival; overall-survival and global applicability remain less mature.

Evidence maturity: Potentially practice-changing.

Practice impact: Possibly soon.

Canadian relevance:
Anbenitamab plus HB1801 was not verified as Health Canada approved or provincially reimbursed. The trial compared against THP, but Canadian first-line HER2-positive metastatic breast cancer standards and access to antibody–drug conjugates are evolving; comparative positioning requires Canadian review.

Importance score: 8.5/10

Key limitations:

* The primary reported benefit is PFS (HR 0.46), not yet established overall-survival improvement.
* Grade ≥3 adverse events were more frequent with the experimental regimen (45.9% vs 34.4%).
* Study setting, evolving first-line options, and availability of the investigational agents may limit Canadian generalizability.

Practical medical relevance:
The absolute 24-month PFS difference was 24.1 percentage points, with higher objective response (87.6% vs 73.4%). That is a substantial signal in HER2-positive metastatic breast cancer, but clinicians should not infer improved survival or replace current Canadian regimens before comparative evidence and authorization.

### 7. Perioperative Durvalumab–Enfortumab Vedotin Shows Better Event-Free Survival in Muscle-Invasive Bladder Cancer

Citation: Powles T, et al. Durvalumab Regimens in Invasive Bladder Cancer.[^7]

Journal/source: New England Journal of Medicine; ESMO 2026

Publication type: Randomized phase III trial report

One-line takeaway: In muscle-invasive bladder cancer, perioperative durvalumab with neoadjuvant enfortumab vedotin improved 24-month event-free survival to 63.5% versus 45.1% with surgery alone in the reported comparison.

Evidence tier: Randomized clinical evidence with a meaningful event-free-survival signal; full subgroup, safety, and survival details require review.

Evidence maturity: Potentially practice-changing.

Practice impact: Possibly soon.

Canadian relevance:
Durvalumab and enfortumab vedotin are used in Canadian oncology for other indications, but Health Canada authorization and provincial funding for this exact perioperative combination were not verified. The trial regimen should not be assumed to be a funded Canadian standard.

Importance score: 8.2/10

Key limitations:

* Event-free survival is reported here; mature overall survival and functional outcomes were not independently verified.
* The available abstract-level summary did not permit complete assessment of toxicity, eligibility, or surgical complication rates.
* The comparator and evolving neoadjuvant standards complicate immediate extrapolation across cisplatin-eligible and cisplatin-ineligible populations.

Practical medical relevance:
Perioperative intensification can prevent recurrence, but the absolute benefit must be balanced against treatment-related toxicity, surgical timing, and access. Multidisciplinary urologic-oncology assessment remains central; the primary manuscript should be checked before local protocol changes.

### 8. Divarasib Reports an Overall-Survival Advantage Against Existing KRAS G12C Inhibitors

Citation: KRASCENDO-1 investigators; Roche. Phase III divarasib versus sotorasib or adagrasib in previously treated KRAS G12C–mutated NSCLC (ESMO 2026 report).[^8]

Journal/source: ESMO 2026; Reuters report of NEJM publication

Publication type: Phase III randomized active-comparator trial, conference presentation and reported journal publication

One-line takeaway: Divarasib reportedly improved median overall survival to approximately 18 months versus 12 months with existing KRAS G12C inhibitors in previously treated NSCLC.

Evidence tier: Potentially high because of randomized active-comparator overall-survival data; full manuscript and DOI not independently retrieved.

Evidence maturity: Potentially practice-changing after detailed peer review and regulatory assessment.

Practice impact: Possibly soon.

Canadian relevance:
Divarasib was not verified as Health Canada approved or reimbursed by October 25. Existing KRAS G12C inhibitor availability and funding vary by Canadian jurisdiction. An ESMO result does not establish Canadian access.

Importance score: 8.2/10

Key limitations:

* The reported death HR was approximately 0.56, but complete prespecified analyses and peer-reviewed source details were not independently verified.
* The 338-patient study applies only to KRAS G12C-mutated, previously treated NSCLC and cannot be generalized to other KRAS variants.
* Treatment sequencing, resistance, crossover, and mature toxicity data remain important.

Practical medical relevance:
Unlike a response-only targeted therapy signal, an active-comparator overall-survival difference could change second-line lung-cancer selection. The reported PFS was 9.8 versus 5.7 months and response rate 58% versus 32%; however, this should be treated as a high-priority result to verify in the final paper rather than an immediate Canadian practice directive.

### 9. Atacicept Adds a Second Strong Two-Year Kidney-Function Signal in IgA Nephropathy

Citation: Barratt J, et al. Atacicept in IgA Nephropathy — Final Efficacy Analysis (ORIGIN 3).[^9]

Journal/source: New England Journal of Medicine; ASN Kidney Week 2026; sponsor-reported final results

Publication type: Final phase III randomized placebo-controlled efficacy analysis

One-line takeaway: Dual BAFF/APRIL inhibition with atacicept stabilized two-year eGFR slope and reduced a composite of kidney-disease progression in IgA nephropathy.

Evidence tier: High for kidney-function endpoints: 428 randomized patients; full primary manuscript details should be consulted.

Evidence maturity: Ready for selected US use for the currently authorized proteinuria indication; broader kidney-protection labeling awaits review.

Practice impact: Now in the US under accelerated approval; possibly soon for broader evidence-based adoption.

Canadian relevance:
Atacicept is FDA accelerated-approved for proteinuria reduction in IgAN, but Health Canada approval and provincial reimbursement were not verified. This week’s two-year results do not automatically broaden Canadian access.

Importance score: 8.0/10

Key limitations:

* Annualized eGFR decline was −0.6 versus −5.6 mL/min/1.73 m²/year, but this is not a direct comparison with sibeprenlimab or other IgAN agents.
* Composite kidney-progression events were 11 versus 38 (HR 0.24); hard dialysis/transplant outcomes remain infrequent.
* Long-term immunoglobulin effects, infection risk, comparative sequencing, and real-world cost are not fully characterized.

Practical medical relevance:
The result independently reinforces B-cell pathway modification as a credible disease-modifying strategy in IgAN. The practical implication is greater urgency for nephrology risk stratification and shared decisions among increasingly numerous therapies, not simultaneous use of multiple untested immunomodulators.

### 10. Ianalumab Meets Statistical Endpoints in Sjögren Disease but Fails to Improve Key Symptoms

Citation: Mariette X, Grader-Beck T, Finzel S, et al. Ianalumab for the Treatment of Patients with Sjögren’s Disease.[^10]

Journal/source: New England Journal of Medicine

Publication type: Two 52-week double-blind randomized placebo-controlled phase III trials

One-line takeaway: Monthly BAFF-receptor–directed B-cell depletion yielded statistically significant but small disease-activity-score improvements without clear symptom or response-rate benefits.

Evidence tier: High-quality trials with modest and clinically uncertain treatment effect.

Evidence maturity: Awaiting replication or implementation; clinical value not yet established.

Practice impact: Unlikely to change routine practice immediately.

Canadian relevance:
Ianalumab is not verified as Health Canada approved for Sjögren disease; Canadian reimbursement is unverified. Symptom-focused management remains central.

Importance score: 7.3/10

Key limitations:

* In NEPTUNUS-1 and NEPTUNUS-2, mean ESSDAI treatment differences were only −1.3 and −1.0 points, with borderline P values (0.0496 and 0.04).
* No significant improvement in prespecified ESSDAI responder rates or patient-reported symptom scores.
* Injection-related reactions were more common; clinically meaningful benefit for dryness, pain, fatigue, or organ-specific disease remains uncertain.

Practical medical relevance:
This is an important example of why a positive primary endpoint is not automatically a practice-changing result. Across 779 participants, the drug altered a composite disease-activity score but did not clearly make patients feel or function better. Regulatory enthusiasm should be proportionate to the absolute effect and patient-centered outcomes.

## Safety, Regulation, and Public Health

### 11. Bundibugyo Ebola Reaches Nigeria as Transmission Continues in the DRC

Citation: World Health Organization. Ebola disease caused by Bundibugyo virus — Democratic Republic of the Congo, Kenya and Nigeria. Disease Outbreak News, October 23, 2026.[^11]

Journal/source: World Health Organization

Publication type: Official infectious-disease outbreak surveillance and public-health advisory

One-line takeaway: WHO reported 9,704 confirmed cases and 4,687 deaths in the DRC by October 20, with imported disease in Nigeria and a linked nine-case cluster in Kenya.

Evidence tier: Authoritative outbreak surveillance, subject to reporting delays and case reconciliation.

Evidence maturity: Ready for public-health action.

Practice impact: Now.

Canadian relevance:
The Canadian public risk remains low, but clinicians should elicit relevant travel and exposure histories, use immediate isolation and appropriate PPE for suspected viral hemorrhagic fever, and contact local/provincial public health urgently. No licensed Bundibugyo-specific vaccine is available for routine Canadian use.

Importance score: 9.0/10

Key limitations:

* The crude case-fatality ratio of 48.3% is not adjusted for ascertainment or care access.
* Kenya reported nine cases with one death, all within a known cluster; Nigeria reported one imported case, not evidence of widespread local transmission.
* WHO advises against blanket travel or trade restrictions; response should focus on targeted surveillance, contact tracing, infection prevention, and care access.

Practical medical relevance:
The DRC epidemic spans 66 health zones in eight provinces; 307 of 482 deaths since the prior WHO update occurred in communities. Kenya traced and quarantined 135 contacts, while Nigeria was monitoring 44. For Canadian clinicians, an exposure-informed differential and rapid escalation are more important than indiscriminate testing or travel bans.

### 12. Health Canada Recalls i-STAT High-Sensitivity Troponin I Cartridges for False-Positive Risk

Citation: Health Canada. i-STAT hs-TnI Cartridge, Class II medical-device recall (RA-82840).[^12]

Journal/source: Health Canada recalls and safety alerts

Publication type: Canadian medical-device recall / analytical performance safety notice

One-line takeaway: Certain Abbott i-STAT hs-TnI cartridges may yield falsely elevated troponin I because of reduced mitigation of macrotroponin and heterophile-antibody interference.

Evidence tier: Official device safety finding with direct diagnostic relevance.

Evidence maturity: Ready for practice.

Practice impact: Now.

Canadian relevance:
This is a **Canadian** Class II recall published October 21, 2026, affecting specified lots beginning A24, A25, B25, B26, C26, or D26 (catalogue 09P81-25). Facilities should coordinate with laboratory medicine, point-of-care testing leadership, and the manufacturer; reimbursement is not relevant.

Importance score: 8.7/10

Key limitations:

* The notice identifies potential falsely **high**, not falsely low, results; actual incident frequency was not quantified.
* The risk is lot-specific and depends on particular analytical interferences, not all high-sensitivity troponin methods.
* Clinical interpretation still requires symptoms, ECG, serial change, and the full clinical context; repeat testing on an unaffected platform may be needed.

Practical medical relevance:
A falsely elevated troponin can trigger unnecessary ACS admission, anticoagulation, angiography, and patient anxiety. Emergency physicians and laboratories should verify cartridge lots, follow local recall instructions, and reconsider discordant point-of-care troponin results with confirmatory central-lab testing when clinically appropriate.

## On the Horizon

### 13. Personalized mRNA Neoantigen Therapy Reduces Melanoma Recurrence in Phase III Reporting

Citation: INTerpath-001 investigators; Merck and Moderna. Intismeran autogene (V940/mRNA-4157) plus pembrolizumab after complete resection of high-risk melanoma, phase III results.[^13]

Journal/source: ESMO 2026; trial registry; contemporary medical reporting (full NEJM manuscript pending public verification)

Publication type: Phase III randomized trial; conference/company-reported topline results awaiting accessible full peer-reviewed report

One-line takeaway: Individualized neoantigen mRNA therapy plus pembrolizumab reportedly improved two-year recurrence-free survival to 82.2% versus 70.0% with pembrolizumab alone.

Evidence tier: Promising large randomized trial signal, but full peer-reviewed methods and mature survival outcomes were not independently available.

Evidence maturity: Awaiting publication, regulatory review, and implementation.

Practice impact: Possibly soon, but not routine practice.

Canadian relevance:
Intismeran is not Health Canada approved or publicly reimbursed; pembrolizumab is available for selected melanoma indications. Manufacturing individualized products requires tumor sequencing, rapid neoantigen selection, production capacity, and payer review.

Importance score: 8.4/10

Key limitations:

* The reported recurrence/death HR was 0.58 (95% CI 0.45–0.75) at median 24.6-month follow-up; overall survival is immature.
* Full trial manuscript, adverse-event tables, subgroup robustness, and statistical plan were not independently verified as of October 25.
* The regimen requires bespoke manufacture and was studied in resected high-risk melanoma, not unselected metastatic disease.

Practical medical relevance:
A 12.2-percentage-point two-year recurrence-free-survival difference is clinically interesting and makes personalized therapeutic vaccines credible beyond small phase II studies. However, it is not a preventive melanoma vaccine and should not be offered outside authorized clinical pathways.

### 14. Circular RNA Aquaporin-1 Delivery Offers a Restorative Approach to Radiation-Induced Xerostomia

Citation: Hoffman HT, Quon H, Rassekh CH, Hassan H. In Vivo Aquaporin-1 Circular RNA Therapy for Radiation-Induced Xerostomia.[^14]

Journal/source: New England Journal of Medicine

Publication type: Phase I first-in-human correspondence / small uncontrolled clinical series

One-line takeaway: A locally administered circular RNA encoding aquaporin-1 improved salivary-flow measures in six people with radiation-induced xerostomia, with transient grade 1–2 adverse events.

Evidence tier: Very preliminary human feasibility and pharmacodynamic signal; no controlled efficacy evidence.

Evidence maturity: Early clinical evidence.

Practice impact: Long-term.

Canadian relevance:
RXRG001 is investigational and was not verified as Health Canada authorized or reimbursed. Radiation oncology and survivorship programs should view this as a research direction, not an available intervention.

Importance score: 6.9/10

Key limitations:

* Only six patients; no placebo, active comparator, or robust estimate of clinical effect.
* Changes in measured salivary flow do not yet establish sustained relief of dryness, swallowing dysfunction, dental complications, or quality-of-life impairment.
* Durability, repeat dosing, local tissue effects, and longer-term RNA-delivery safety are unknown.

Practical medical relevance:
This is unusually relevant to head-and-neck cancer survivorship: rather than preventing salivary-gland damage alone, it attempts to restore fluid secretion in irradiated glands. The biological rationale is credible, but the report should prompt controlled trials rather than clinical adoption.

## Hype Check

### 15. AI Diagnostic Orchestration Outperforms Physicians on Written Cases, Not in Clinical Practice

Citation: Nori H, Horvitz E, et al. Microsoft AI Diagnostic Orchestrator sequential-diagnosis evaluation; Science publication reported October 22, 2026.[^15]

Journal/source: Science; Microsoft sequential-diagnosis benchmark; secondary technical reporting

Publication type: Retrospective technical benchmark and simulated clinician comparison

One-line takeaway: A diagnostic AI system reportedly achieved about 83% accuracy on complex written NEJM cases versus about 36% for physicians with references, without prospective evidence of improved patient care.

Evidence tier: Technical benchmark evidence; low clinical effectiveness maturity.

Evidence maturity: Preclinical/conceptual for autonomous clinical deployment.

Practice impact: Unlikely to change practice now.

Canadian relevance:
No Canadian authorization or demonstrated Canadian clinical deployment for this diagnostic orchestrator was verified. Canadian use would require prospective evaluation, privacy and safety governance, workflow integration, professional accountability, and applicable device regulation.

Importance score: 6.7/10

Key limitations:

* NEJM case records are curated, retrospective, and often unusually difficult; they are not representative of undifferentiated primary care or emergency presentations.
* The comparator did not reproduce team-based clinical care, bedside examination, iterative patient communication, or all available diagnostic resources.
* No prospective randomized study demonstrated better diagnostic accuracy, reduced harm, improved outcomes, or cost-effectiveness in real clinical practice.

Practical medical relevance:
The system is technically interesting because it sequences questions and tests rather than merely answering exam-style prompts. But “83% versus 36%” should not be translated into “AI doctors are better than physicians.” The decisive evidence would be prospective, externally validated, safety-monitored studies showing better decisions and patient outcomes within real clinical workflows.

## Weekly Takeaways

**Most likely to change practice:** Dostarlimab for dMMR/MSI-H locally advanced rectal cancer, contingent on expert response assessment and Canadian authorization. For specialists, the tarlatamab–durvalumab survival gain and the IgAN eGFR results may be equally transformative.

**Most important new therapeutic development:** Bepirovirsen, the first FDA-approved antisense therapy for chronic HBV that permits a subset of carefully selected patients to stop chronic antiviral treatment. Serious toxicity and modest absolute functional-cure rates temper enthusiasm.

**Most important safety or public-health update:** The continued Bundibugyo Ebola epidemic and imported Nigerian case; immediately actionable for Canadian emergency departments is the Abbott i-STAT high-sensitivity troponin I false-positive recall.

**Most interesting emerging technology:** Personalized neoantigen mRNA vaccination after melanoma resection, supported by phase III recurrence-free-survival data but awaiting full accessible publication, regulatory review, and a workable manufacturing model.

**Most overhyped story:** AI diagnostic orchestration “beating doctors” on selected written cases. This is a benchmark, not evidence of superior real-world care.

**One development physicians may be underestimating:** Disease-modifying treatment of IgA nephropathy is moving from proteinuria surrogates to convincing two-year eGFR preservation across multiple randomized programs. The challenge will soon be choosing and funding the right regimen.

**What patients may ask about this week:** “Can immunotherapy replace rectal cancer surgery?”, “Is hepatitis B curable now?”, “Can a new weight-loss drug treat sleep apnea?”, “Is a personalized cancer vaccine available?”, and “Can AI diagnose better than my doctor?” Each requires a clear distinction between promising evidence and locally authorized care.
