# Weekly Medical News and Clinical Horizon Scan: September 14--20, 2026

## Bottom Line This Week

The strongest clinical signal was a randomized trial in **newly
diagnosed high-risk pediatric B-cell acute lymphoblastic leukemia
(B-ALL)**: replacing two conventional chemotherapy cycles with
blinatumomab increased estimated 4-year event-free survival from 70.3%
to 83.0% and markedly reduced treatment-related infection. This is the
clearest potentially practice-changing development of the week.

A second major randomized result came from **EPoS**, in which 10,799
patients with high-risk adenomas were assigned to begin surveillance
colonoscopy at 5 or 3 years. Five-year colorectal-cancer incidence was
0.77% versus 0.82%, meeting the interim noninferiority criterion. If the
planned 10-year analysis confirms this result and guidelines change, the
health-system effect could be substantial.

The most important regulatory development was FDA approval of
**finerenone for CKD associated with type 1 diabetes**, the first new US
treatment in this setting in more than three decades. The caveat
matters: FINE-ONE used albuminuria reduction rather than kidney failure
or cardiovascular events as its primary endpoint.

The most important new rare-disease therapy was **Fayuvi**, a one-time
systemic AAV9 gene therapy and the first FDA-approved treatment for
Sanfilippo syndrome type A. Evidence comes from a single-arm study with
historical controls, long-term safety remains uncertain, and the
reported US price of approximately US\$3.95 million will constrain
access.

For Canada, Health Canada authorized the **XFG-adapted 2026--2027
Comirnaty COVID-19 vaccine for people aged 6 months and older**;
provincial eligibility and public funding remain jurisdiction-specific.
Globally, Bundibugyo Ebola continued to expand in the Democratic
Republic of the Congo, exceeding 7,400 confirmed cases and 3,600 deaths
by September 16.

The most credible emerging science was **levetiracetam as a possible
cancer-neuroscience therapy in diffuse midline glioma (DMG)**.
Mechanistic and preclinical data are compelling and retrospective
clinical cohorts suggest longer survival, but there is no prospective
therapeutic trial.

Overall, this was a **clinically important week**. Blinatumomab
substitution and less-intensive colonoscopy surveillance are the
strongest patient-centred randomized signals; the regulatory advances
are consequential but require careful separation of hard-outcome
evidence from surrogate- or historical-control--based approvals.

## Must Know

### 1. Blinatumomab Replacing Two Chemotherapy Cycles Improves Event-Free Survival in High-Risk Pediatric B-ALL

Citation: AIEOP-BFM ALL 2017 investigators. Blinatumomab for Replacing
Chemotherapy in Pediatric Acute Lymphoblastic Leukemia.[^1]

Journal/source: New England Journal of Medicine

Publication type: Multicentre randomized controlled trial

One-line takeaway: Replacing two post-consolidation chemotherapy cycles
with blinatumomab increased estimated 4-year event-free survival from
70.3% to 83.0%.

Evidence tier: High-quality randomized clinical-outcome evidence.

Evidence maturity: Potentially practice-changing.

Practice impact: Possibly soon.

Canadian relevance: Blinatumomab is available in Canada for selected
B-cell precursor ALL indications, but this specific frontline
chemotherapy-replacement strategy will require cooperative-group
adoption, regulatory alignment, provincial funding, and infusion
capacity. Approval of this exact strategy in Canada could not be
verified as of September 20, 2026.

Importance score: 10/10

Key limitations:

-   Median follow-up was 2.9 years at this planned interim analysis.
-   Neurotoxic events were more frequent with blinatumomab despite fewer
    life-threatening adverse events overall.
-   Results apply to the specific high-risk B-ALL population and
    protocol studied.

Practical medical relevance: Among 709 randomized patients, estimated
4-year event-free survival was 83.0% with blinatumomab versus 70.3% with
chemotherapy (HR 0.51). Treatment-related infection fell from 69.4% to
23.9%. This is an important oncology paradigm: immunotherapy is
beginning to **replace toxic chemotherapy while improving efficacy**,
rather than simply being layered on top of it.

### 2. Five-Year Surveillance Is Noninferior to Three-Year Colonoscopy After High-Risk Adenoma Removal

Citation: Jover R, Bretthauer M, Cubiella J, et al; EPoS Study Group.
Colonoscopy Intervals and Colorectal Cancer Incidence after Adenoma
Removal.[^2]

Journal/source: New England Journal of Medicine

Publication type: Large multinational randomized noninferiority trial;
planned interim analysis

One-line takeaway: Beginning surveillance colonoscopy at 5 years rather
than 3 years after removal of high-risk adenomas was noninferior for
5-year colorectal-cancer incidence.

Evidence tier: Large randomized comparative-effectiveness evidence using
cancer incidence as the key endpoint.

Evidence maturity: Potentially practice-changing, pending final 10-year
analysis and guideline review.

Practice impact: Possibly soon.

Canadian relevance: Three-year surveillance remains common after
advanced or multiple adenomas in Canada. These results could eventually
lengthen intervals and reduce colonoscopy demand, but practice should
await local guideline review and the planned final analysis.

Importance score: 9/10

Key limitations:

-   This is an interim analysis of a 10-year trial.
-   Participants were enrolled in European screening and endoscopy
    systems.
-   Safe interval extension assumes a high-quality baseline colonoscopy
    and complete polyp removal.

Practical medical relevance: Among 10,799 randomized patients, 5-year
colorectal-cancer incidence was 0.77% with first surveillance at 5 years
and 0.82% with first surveillance at 3 years. Only five
colorectal-cancer deaths occurred. If durable, extending surveillance
could reduce procedural harms, patient burden, cost, and endoscopy wait
lists without sacrificing cancer prevention.

### 3. FDA Approves Finerenone for CKD Associated With Type 1 Diabetes---but on a Surrogate Endpoint

Citation: US Food and Drug Administration; FINE-ONE investigators.
Finerenone for chronic kidney disease associated with type 1
diabetes.[^3]

Journal/source: US Food and Drug Administration; New England Journal of
Medicine

Publication type: Regulatory indication expansion supported by a
randomized phase III surrogate-endpoint trial

One-line takeaway: Finerenone became the first new FDA-approved
treatment in more than 30 years for adults with CKD associated with type
1 diabetes, based primarily on reduction in albuminuria.

Evidence tier: Randomized phase III evidence for a kidney-risk
surrogate, supported by hard-outcome data from type 2 diabetes.

Evidence maturity: Ready for US practice; hard renal-outcome
confirmation in type 1 diabetes remains limited.

Practice impact: Now in the United States.

Canadian relevance: Finerenone is available in Canada for CKD associated
with type 2 diabetes, but Health Canada authorization for CKD associated
with type 1 diabetes could not be verified as of September 20, 2026. FDA
approval does not automatically change Canadian prescribing or
reimbursement.

Importance score: 9/10

Key limitations:

-   FINE-ONE enrolled 242 participants and used 6-month UACR change
    rather than kidney failure, sustained eGFR decline, cardiovascular
    events, or mortality.
-   Clinical benefit is partly inferred from the albuminuria--outcome
    relationship established in type 2 diabetes.
-   Hyperkalemia and renal-function monitoring remain important.

Practical medical relevance: Finerenone reduced UACR by approximately
25% relative to placebo. The approval addresses a genuine therapeutic
gap, but the correct interpretation is "FDA-authorized on a validated
surrogate with supportive class evidence," not "proven to prevent kidney
failure in type 1 diabetes."

## Important Developments

### 4. FDA Approves the First Gene Therapy for Sanfilippo Syndrome Type A

Citation: US Food and Drug Administration. FDA Approves First Gene
Therapy for Pediatric Patients with Sanfilippo Syndrome Type A.[^4]

Journal/source: US Food and Drug Administration

Publication type: Regulatory approval based on an open-label single-arm
clinical study with historical controls

One-line takeaway: Fayuvi became the first approved disease-modifying
treatment for MPS IIIA, delivering a functional SGSH gene through a
one-time intravenous AAV9 vector.

Evidence tier: Rare-disease clinical evidence with biologic plausibility
and neurodevelopmental benefit versus historical natural-history
controls.

Evidence maturity: Ready for selected US patients with substantial
residual uncertainty.

Practice impact: Now in the United States.

Canadian relevance: Health Canada authorization and Canadian
reimbursement could not be verified as of September 20, 2026. Canadian
access would require specialized gene-therapy infrastructure, regulatory
review, provincial funding, and management of very high acquisition
costs.

Importance score: 8/10

Key limitations:

-   The pivotal study was single-arm and used historical controls.
-   Long-term durability and late AAV-related safety remain incompletely
    characterized.
-   The reported US list price is approximately US\$3.95 million.

Practical medical relevance: MPS IIIA causes progressive heparan-sulfate
accumulation and severe neurocognitive decline. Fayuvi attempts to
restore sulfamidase activity through systemic AAV9-mediated SGSH gene
delivery. This is a genuine first-in-disease option and another test of
how health systems evaluate ultra-high-cost one-time gene therapies
supported by nonrandomized rare-disease evidence.

### 5. Imlunestrant Plus Abemaciclib Gains Full FDA Approval After Endocrine Progression in ESR1-Mutant Breast Cancer

Citation: US Food and Drug Administration. FDA approves imlunestrant in
combination with abemaciclib for ER-positive, HER2-negative,
ESR1-mutated advanced or metastatic breast cancer.[^5]

Journal/source: US Food and Drug Administration; EMBER-3

Publication type: Regulatory approval supported by a randomized phase
III trial

One-line takeaway: In ESR1-mutant advanced breast cancer after endocrine
progression, imlunestrant plus abemaciclib approximately doubled median
PFS versus imlunestrant alone.

Evidence tier: Randomized phase III progression-free-survival evidence.

Evidence maturity: Ready for US practice.

Practice impact: Now in the United States.

Canadian relevance: Health Canada authorization for this combination
could not be verified as of September 20, 2026. Canadian uptake would
also depend on validated ESR1 testing and provincial reimbursement.

Importance score: 8/10

Key limitations:

-   The ESR1-mutant combination comparison involved 159 patients.
-   Median PFS was 11.1 versus 5.5 months; mature overall-survival
    benefit is not established.
-   Abemaciclib adds diarrhea, neutropenia, hepatotoxicity, venous
    thromboembolism, and ILD/pneumonitis risk.

Practical medical relevance: This adds another evidence-based option for
ESR1-mutant endocrine-resistant disease. It is conceptually distinct
from the previous week's camizestrant approval: EMBER-3 supports
changing therapy **after clinical progression**, whereas SERENA-6 tested
molecular switching before radiographic progression.

### 6. Levacetylleucine Becomes the First FDA-Approved Treatment for Ataxia in Ataxia-Telangiectasia

Citation: US Food and Drug Administration. FDA Approves Therapy to Treat
Ataxia in Patients with Ataxia-Telangiectasia, a Rare Genetic
Disorder.[^6]

Journal/source: US Food and Drug Administration; Lancet Neurology

Publication type: Regulatory indication expansion supported by a phase
III randomized double-blind placebo-controlled crossover trial

One-line takeaway: Levacetylleucine improved ataxia scores over 12 weeks
and became the first FDA-approved treatment for neurologic dysfunction
in ataxia-telangiectasia.

Evidence tier: Randomized phase III evidence in a rare neurodegenerative
disease.

Evidence maturity: Ready for US practice; long-term disease modification
remains unknown.

Practice impact: Now in the United States.

Canadian relevance: Health Canada authorization for
ataxia-telangiectasia could not be verified as of September 20, 2026.
The drug does not address the immune deficiency or cancer predisposition
associated with ATM deficiency.

Importance score: 7/10

Key limitations:

-   Only 73 patients were randomized.
-   Each treatment period lasted 12 weeks.
-   The endpoint was an ataxia scale rather than independence,
    hospitalization, malignancy, or survival.

Practical medical relevance: The crossover trial showed a treatment
effect of approximately −1.9 points on SARA versus placebo, without
treatment-related serious adverse events or deaths. For a disorder with
no prior approved neurologic therapy, symptomatic improvement is
meaningful, but this is not correction of the multisystem consequences
of ATM deficiency.

## Safety, Regulation, and Public Health

### 7. Health Canada Authorizes the XFG-Adapted Comirnaty Vaccine for the 2026--2027 Season

Citation: Health Canada; Pfizer Canada and BioNTech. Authorization of
XFG variant-adapted Comirnaty for the 2026--2027 COVID-19 season.[^7]

Journal/source: Health Canada authorization; Pfizer Canada

Publication type: Seasonal vaccine regulatory authorization

One-line takeaway: Canada authorized the XFG-adapted Comirnaty
formulation for active immunization against COVID-19 in people aged 6
months and older.

Evidence tier: Regulatory update based on accumulated mRNA-vaccine
evidence plus updated nonclinical, immunogenicity, manufacturing, and
variant-matching data.

Evidence maturity: Ready for Canadian use.

Practice impact: Now.

Canadian relevance: Directly applicable. Health Canada authorized the
vaccine from 6 months of age. Actual publicly funded eligibility,
timing, and delivery remain dependent on NACI and provincial or
territorial programs.

Importance score: 8/10

Key limitations:

-   Variant-updated authorization does not mean clinical-outcome
    efficacy against every circulating lineage has been directly
    measured in a new randomized trial.
-   Protection against infection wanes and differs from protection
    against severe disease.
-   Provincial eligibility and funding can be narrower than the Health
    Canada product indication.

Practical medical relevance: This is the week's most immediate Canadian
seasonal-practice update. The key distinction is between **regulatory
authorization** and **program eligibility**; clinicians should use local
provincial recommendations when advising patients about access and
timing.

### 8. Bundibugyo Ebola Epidemic Continues to Expand in the DRC

Citation: World Health Organization; European Centre for Disease
Prevention and Control. Bundibugyo Ebola outbreak updates, Democratic
Republic of the Congo.[^8]

Journal/source: WHO and ECDC outbreak surveillance

Publication type: Public-health outbreak surveillance and emergency
response update

One-line takeaway: By September 16, the DRC had reported approximately
7,475 confirmed Bundibugyo Ebola cases and 3,605 deaths, with intense
transmission in Ituri and North Kivu.

Evidence tier: Official outbreak surveillance data.

Evidence maturity: Actionable public-health information; counts remain
subject to reconciliation.

Practice impact: Now.

Canadian relevance: Direct risk to most Canadian clinicians remains low,
but travel and exposure histories matter. There is no licensed vaccine
with proven effectiveness against Bundibugyo virus; suspected imported
cases require immediate infection-prevention and public-health
escalation.

Importance score: 8/10

Key limitations:

-   Case and death totals are continually revised.
-   Transmission intensity varies substantially by province and health
    zone.
-   Conflict, mobility, health-system disruption, and attacks on health
    workers complicate response.

Practical medical relevance: The epidemic is now a major regional
emergency. Vaccine and therapeutic trials are accelerating, but standard
Ebola infection prevention, rapid isolation, contact tracing, and
supportive care remain central.

## On the Horizon

### 9. Levetiracetam May Directly Disrupt Neuron-to-Glioma Signaling in Diffuse Midline Glioma

Citation: Barron T, Drexler R, Mochizuki A, et al. Levetiracetam
therapeutically targets GABAergic synapses in diffuse midline
glioma.[^9]

Journal/source: Nature Medicine

Publication type: Translational mechanistic study combining
retrospective clinical cohorts and preclinical models

One-line takeaway: Levetiracetam was associated with longer survival in
retrospective DMG cohorts and suppressed tumor growth in preclinical
models by attenuating GABAergic neuron-to-glioma synaptic signaling.

Evidence tier: Strong mechanistic and preclinical evidence with
retrospective human association; no prospective therapeutic trial.

Evidence maturity: Early clinical evidence.

Practice impact: Long-term.

Canadian relevance: Levetiracetam is inexpensive and widely available in
Canada, making repurposing attractive. There is no evidence supporting
routine initiation solely for antitumor benefit in DMG; a prospective
trial is required.

Importance score: 8/10

Key limitations:

-   Human survival evidence is retrospective and confounded.
-   Much of the causal evidence comes from xenografts and experimental
    electrophysiology.
-   The effect appeared subtype-specific and was not reproduced in
    hemispheric H3/IDH-wild-type high-grade glioma models.

Practical medical relevance: This is a compelling example of **cancer
neuroscience** becoming therapeutically testable. DMG cells receive
growth-promoting synaptic input from neurons; levetiracetam appears to
reduce a GABAergic component through a mechanism distinct from its
canonical SV2A antiseizure action. Prospective testing is warranted, but
retrospective association is not treatment efficacy.

## Hype Check

### 10. CELESTIMO's Follicular-Lymphoma "Phase III Win" Is Still Press-Release-Only

Citation: Roche. Lunsumio-based regimen significantly improves
progression-free survival in follicular lymphoma in phase III CELESTIMO
study.[^10]

Journal/source: Roche/Genentech company announcement; ClinicalTrials.gov
NCT04712097

Publication type: Company topline phase III press release

One-line takeaway: Mosunetuzumab plus lenalidomide reportedly improved
PFS over rituximab plus lenalidomide, but no effect size or mature
survival data have been disclosed.

Evidence tier: Potentially important phase III result, currently
uninterpretable beyond the sponsor's topline claim.

Evidence maturity: Awaiting full presentation, peer review, and
regulatory assessment.

Practice impact: Unlikely to change practice immediately.

Canadian relevance: Mosunetuzumab is available in Canada in selected
relapsed/refractory follicular-lymphoma settings, but this earlier-line
combination should not be treated as an established Canadian standard.
Authorization and reimbursement for the CELESTIMO regimen could not be
verified.

Importance score: 6/10

Key limitations:

-   No hazard ratio, median PFS, absolute benefit, subgroup results, or
    detailed adverse-event rates were disclosed.
-   Overall-survival data were immature.
-   Public efficacy information is currently sponsor-reported only.

Practical medical relevance: The study may ultimately matter because an
outpatient CD20×CD3 bispecific-plus-lenalidomide regimen could move
bispecific therapy earlier in follicular lymphoma. For now, a positive
phase III press release is insufficient to compare efficacy, toxicity,
quality of life, or value.

## Weekly Takeaways

**Most likely to change practice:** Replacing two chemotherapy cycles
with blinatumomab in high-risk pediatric B-ALL.

**Most important new therapeutic development:** Fayuvi for Sanfilippo
syndrome type A---the first disease-modifying therapy for this
devastating pediatric neurodegenerative disorder, albeit with major
evidence and access caveats.

**Most important safety or public-health update:** Health Canada's
authorization of the XFG-adapted 2026--2027 Comirnaty formulation;
globally, the Bundibugyo Ebola epidemic remains the more severe
emergency.

**Most interesting emerging technology:** Cancer-neuron synapse
targeting. The levetiracetam DMG study suggests a familiar neurologic
drug may alter tumor biology by interrupting electrical communication
between neurons and malignant cells.

**Most overhyped story:** The CELESTIMO follicular-lymphoma result. It
may become important, but "phase III positive" without an effect size,
mature survival, detailed safety, or peer-reviewed data is not enough
for clinical conclusions.

**One development physicians may be underestimating:** Five-year
colonoscopy surveillance after high-risk adenoma removal. A safe
two-year extension could have enormous cumulative effects on endoscopy
capacity, procedural harms, and health-system spending.

**What patients may ask about this week:** Whether some children with
leukemia can receive less chemotherapy, whether follow-up colonoscopy
really needs to occur every three years, whether finerenone protects
kidneys in type 1 diabetes, whether the new seasonal COVID vaccine is
available in Canada, and whether a seizure medication can slow an
aggressive childhood brain tumour.
