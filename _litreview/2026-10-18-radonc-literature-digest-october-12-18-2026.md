# Weekly Radiation Oncology Literature Surveillance Digest: October 12–18, 2026

## Bottom Line This Week

This is a **reconstructed edition** based on the previously generated October 18 digest. The original attachment could not be recovered, so this version contains the four key publications that could be independently verified. It is **not** a byte-for-byte replacement of the previous nine-paper edition.

The clinically most consequential signal was negative: the phase II **SAABR** trial combining prostate SBRT, atezolizumab, androgen-deprivation therapy, and an androgen-receptor pathway inhibitor in metastatic hormone-sensitive prostate cancer closed early after severe toxicity, including two grade 4 colonic perforations and one treatment-related death from hemophagocytic lymphohistiocytosis. No convincing incremental benefit over historical controls was demonstrated. Importantly, the fatal toxicity occurred before hormonal therapy or SBRT; causality should not be assigned to the radiation component alone.

The prospective phase I/II **F-SHARP** study reported encouraging feasibility of dose-escalated focal salvage HDR brachytherapy in radiorecurrent prostate cancer. Among 62 participants, late grade 3 toxicity occurred in two patients (3.2%) and 3-year biochemical progression-free survival was 62.7%. These outcomes are promising for selected patients but lack randomized comparison.

For thoracic practice, the **American Radium Society Appropriate Use Criteria** address accelerated hypofractionation for patients with locally advanced NSCLC who cannot receive concurrent chemotherapy. The document is particularly useful for interpreting short-course dose constraints and the sequencing of immunotherapy; it does not establish superiority of accelerated schedules over conventional fractionation.

The strongest operational paper used **statistical process control** applied to routine oncology information-system records to identify linac downtime and patient redistribution across four clinics. This is a low-overhead use of existing departmental data, although detection sensitivity was only 0.70 against incident logs.

Overall, this was a **moderately important week**, with a meaningful treatment-safety warning, useful prospective salvage-brachytherapy evidence, a practical thoracic consensus document, and workflow-quality improvement. No new randomized efficacy trial clearly established a universal standard of care.

## Must Read

### 1. Phase II Study of an Androgen Receptor Pathway Inhibitor, Atezolizumab, ADT, and Prostate SBRT in Metastatic Hormone-Sensitive Prostate Cancer

Citation: SAABR trial investigators. Phase II Study of an Androgen Receptor Pathway Inhibitor + Atezolizumab + Gonadotropin-Releasing Hormone Analog and Stereotactic Body Radiotherapy in Hormone-Sensitive Metastatic Prostate Cancer. *JCO Oncology Advances*. Published October 2026.[^1]

Journal/source: JCO Oncology Advances  
Publication type: Peer-reviewed multicentre phase II clinical trial  
One-line takeaway: The addition of atezolizumab to intensified hormonal therapy and prostate SBRT caused substantial toxicity without clear incremental benefit.

Evidence tier: Prospective single-arm phase II safety and efficacy evidence; trial stopped early.

Importance score: 9/10

Key limitations:

- Only 28 participants were enrolled, and there was no randomized control group.
- Comparison with historical outcomes is unreliable across different disease-volume and systemic-therapy populations.
- The fatal immune toxicity occurred before prostate SBRT, so it should not be attributed to radiation.
- The two colonic perforations occurred near presumed radiation overlap, but precise causal attribution is difficult in a multimodality regimen.

Practical radiation oncology relevance:
The study is a caution against assuming that adding immune checkpoint blockade to intensified hormonal therapy and prostate SBRT is necessarily synergistic or safe. At two years, freedom from failure was 71%, but 46% experienced grade 3 or higher treatment-related adverse events; the trial closed after two grade 4 colonic perforations and one fatal hemophagocytic lymphohistiocytosis event. It does not invalidate established prostate-directed RT in appropriately selected metastatic patients; rather, it argues against routine adoption of this investigational combination.

### 2. Dose-Escalated Focal Salvage High-Dose-Rate Brachytherapy and Genomic Classifier Risk Stratification in Radiorecurrent Prostate Cancer: The F-SHARP Phase I/II Clinical Trial

Citation: F-SHARP trial investigators. Dose-Escalated Focal Salvage High-Dose-Rate Brachytherapy and Genomic Classifier Risk Stratification in Radiorecurrent Prostate Cancer: The F-SHARP Phase I/II Clinical Trial. *Journal of Clinical Oncology*. Published October 2026.[^2]

Journal/source: Journal of Clinical Oncology  
Publication type: Peer-reviewed prospective phase I/II clinical trial  
One-line takeaway: Focal salvage HDR brachytherapy was feasible with 3.2% late grade 3 toxicity and 62.7% three-year biochemical progression-free survival in a 62-patient cohort.

Evidence tier: Prospective single-arm early-phase clinical evidence.

Importance score: 8/10

Key limitations:

- Small sample and no randomized comparison with salvage prostatectomy, cryotherapy, SBRT, or observation.
- Selection of patients with localized radiorecurrence limits generalizability.
- The genomic-classifier analysis covered only 31 patients and remains exploratory.
- Longer follow-up is needed for very late urinary and rectal toxicity.

Practical radiation oncology relevance:
The trial supports focal HDR brachytherapy as a specialized salvage option in carefully restaged radiorecurrent prostate cancer. Twenty-four patients received one fraction and 38 received two; no acute grade 3 toxicity was reported. Three-year radiographic PFS was 67.5%, local recurrence-free survival 73.0%, and metastasis-free survival 85.9%. A higher genomic-classifier score was associated with poorer outcomes, but this is not yet sufficient to assign treatment intensification.

## Worth Skimming

### 3. American Radium Society Appropriate Use Criteria: Accelerated Hypofractionated Radiotherapy in Locally Advanced NSCLC Ineligible for Concurrent Chemotherapy

Citation: Chun SG, Iyengar P, Rimner A, Amini A, Donington J, Edelman MJ, Feigenberg SJ, Geng Y, Gubens MA, Higgins KA, Juloori A, Ning MS, Park HS, Slotman BJ, Wolf AS, Simone CB II. American Radium Society Appropriate Use Criteria: Accelerated Hypofractionated Radiotherapy in the Multidisciplinary Management of Locally-Advanced Non-Small Cell Lung Cancer Ineligible for Concurrent Chemotherapy. *International Journal of Radiation Oncology, Biology, Physics*. Published online October 13, 2026.[^3]

Journal/source: International Journal of Radiation Oncology, Biology, Physics  
Publication type: Peer-reviewed multidisciplinary appropriate-use consensus guidance  
One-line takeaway: Accelerated hypofractionation is a reasonable option in selected chemotherapy-ineligible locally advanced NSCLC, but conventional 60 Gy in 30 fractions remains appropriate.

Evidence tier: Expert consensus informed by systematic literature review and modified Delphi methodology.

Importance score: 7.5/10

Key limitations:

- The guidance is consensus-based rather than a new randomized comparison of fractionation strategies.
- Tumour proximity to the esophagus and brachial plexus materially constrains safe escalation.
- Most underlying trials are early-phase or observational.
- Immunotherapy sequencing recommendations may evolve with future prospective trials.

Practical radiation oncology relevance:
The panel supported modern conformal therapy and suggested constraints of lung V20Gy below 30% and heart V40Gy below 20% with accelerated hypofractionation. For 15-fraction courses near the esophagus or brachial plexus, escalation beyond 45–55 Gy was considered inappropriate. Consolidative immunotherapy was viewed favorably whereas concurrent immunotherapy was considered inappropriate in these scenarios. This is useful guidance for older, frail, or comorbid patients with stage III NSCLC who are unable to receive concurrent chemotherapy.

### 4. Automated Linac Downtime Tracking Across Four Radiation Oncology Clinics Using Statistical Process Control

Citation: Serafini CE, Thomas M, Yang X, Patel P, Roper J, Washington M, Dhabaan A, Al-Hallaq H. Automated linac downtime tracking across 4 radiation oncology clinics within a single hospital system utilizing statistical process control. *Journal of Applied Clinical Medical Physics*. 2026;27(10):e70863.[^4]

Journal/source: Journal of Applied Clinical Medical Physics  
Publication type: Peer-reviewed retrospective quality-improvement and workflow validation study  
One-line takeaway: Routinely collected scheduling and treatment data can automatically flag linac outages and reveal workload redistribution across machines.

Evidence tier: Multi-clinic retrospective operational validation across 10 linacs over 29 months.

Importance score: 7/10

Key limitations:

- All four clinics were within one hospital system using a shared oncology information system.
- Sensitivity was only 0.70 against existing event logs.
- Reported outages are an imperfect ground-truth reference.
- Demonstrated detection does not yet prove reduced treatment delays or improved patient outcomes.

Practical radiation oncology relevance:
The approach used machine throughput, operating hours, and cancellations to identify unexpected disruptions. Against 138 documented downtime events, it identified additional unlogged events with operational patterns suggestive of machine interruption. Specificity was approximately 0.99. The method could help departments quantify equipment reliability and justify contingency capacity using existing scheduling data.
