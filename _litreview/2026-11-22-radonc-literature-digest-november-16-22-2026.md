# Weekly Radiation Oncology Literature Surveillance Digest: November 16–22, 2026

## Bottom Line This Week

The strongest clinical signal is the mature CROP prospective trial of comprehensive SBRT with intermittent ADT for oligometastatic hormone-sensitive prostate cancer. Long follow-up is valuable, but the absence of a control arm prevents conclusions about survival benefit or whether intermittent treatment is preferable to contemporary systemic intensification. Five patients developed vertebral fractures.[^1]

A historical comparison of HDR prostate boosts supports the feasibility of a single 15 Gy fraction but does not establish equivalence to two fractions. The most operationally relevant physics study addresses bowel motion during MR-guided adaptive abdominal SBRT: adaptation before delivery does not eliminate subsequent anatomical uncertainty.[^2][^3]

The DE-RADIATE protocol is an interesting biomarker-directed head-and-neck research effort, not an indication to reduce routine treatment doses. A new editorial revisits intracranial tile brachytherapy, but the underlying ROADS phase III publication is from September, not this week.[^4][^5]

Overall importance: moderate, principally implementation and hypothesis generation. No new practice-changing randomized result or externally validated clinical AI paper was verified in the retrieved material. This is not evidence that none was published.

Surveillance scope: November 16–22 inclusive, prioritizing first online publication over subsequent issue assignment. Web searches targeted PubMed, arXiv, medRxiv, bioRxiv, IJROBP, Radiotherapy & Oncology, Practical Radiation Oncology, ASCO/JCO, Lancet Oncology, Nature Medicine, Medical Physics and Physics in Medicine & Biology. Coverage was incomplete: direct IOP access was blocked and the searches did not yield verifiable in-window candidates from several requested sources. Appraisals primarily use abstracts and available publisher material, not universal full-text review. Scores are editorial judgments of clinical significance, evidence quality and practical usefulness, not validated metrics.

## Must Read

### 1. Intermittent Androgen Deprivation Therapy Plus Comprehensive Stereotactic Body Radiotherapy for Oligometastatic Hormone Sensitive Prostate Cancer (CROP): Mature Results of a Prospective Trial

Citation: Cheung P, Tseng CL, Chung H, Chu W, Vesprini D, Liu S, Sahgal A, Soliman H, Myrehaug S, Detsky J, Chung P, Helou J, Emmenegger U, Escueta V, Mamedov A, Zhang L, Morton G, Loblaw A. Int J Radiat Oncol Biol Phys. 2026 Nov 16. Online ahead of print.[^1]

Journal/source: International Journal of Radiation Oncology, Biology, Physics  
Publication type: Peer-reviewed single-arm phase I/II prospective trial  
One-line takeaway: Long-term follow-up illustrates the potential for treatment-free intervals after comprehensive metastasis-directed RT combined with finite ADT.

Evidence tier: Prospective nonrandomized study; 89 evaluable patients, median follow-up 74.7 months.

Importance score: 8/10

Key limitations:

- No randomized comparator to isolate the effect of SBRT or intermittent ADT.
- Conventional imaging eligibility limits extrapolation to PSMA PET-defined disease.
- Contemporary systemic intensification may change the benefit-risk balance.

Practical radiation oncology relevance:
Five-year failure-free and overall survival were 15.2% and 78.2%, respectively; 39.3% were alive with hormone-sensitive disease and off ADT at last follow-up. Severe adverse events occurred in six patients, including five vertebral fractures. The study is useful for discussing treatment-free intervals and spinal SBRT risks, but does not justify replacing established systemic treatment strategies.

## Worth Skimming

### 2. Single- Versus Two-Fraction HDR Brachytherapy Boost for Prostate Cancer: Long-Term Oncologic and Toxicity Outcomes

Citation: Goñi A, Sevilla M, Larruskain M, Eguiguren M, Perez A, Sáenz de Urturi E, Iceta U, Roura DA, Alberich M, Minguez J, Urraca JM, Querejeta A. Pract Radiat Oncol. 2026 Nov 18. Online ahead of print.[^2]

Journal/source: Practical Radiation Oncology  
Publication type: Peer-reviewed retrospective comparative cohort  
One-line takeaway: Long-term disease control appeared similar between historical single- and two-fraction HDR boost protocols, without proof of equivalence.

Evidence tier: Nonrandomized cohort of 378 patients, median follow-up 130.1 months.

Importance score: 7/10

Key limitations:

- Treatment era and sequencing of EBRT differed between groups.
- Baseline risk characteristics were imbalanced.
- No formal equivalence test; uncertainty remains substantial.

Practical radiation oncology relevance:
Ten-year biochemical recurrence-free survival was 79.6% with one fraction versus 82.6% with two (p=0.784). Crude grade 3 or higher GU toxicity was 10.4% versus 22.9%. These findings are relevant to procedural capacity and patient burden, but historical confounding prevents attributing the toxicity difference to fractionation alone. The results concern boost therapy with EBRT, not HDR monotherapy.

### 3. Intrafraction Organs-at-Risk Volume and Dose Variations in Upper Abdominal Adaptive Stereotactic Body Radiotherapy on 0.35 T Magnetic Resonance-Guided Linear Accelerator

Citation: Beige A, Thibouw D, Tachin-Bourgeon C, Chevalier C, Truc G, Baude J, Aubignac L, Peignaux K, Petitfils A, Boudet J, Rouffiac M, Bertaut A, Nunes P, Bessières I. Cancer Radiother. 2026;30(7):104881. Published November 19.[^3]

Journal/source: Cancer Radiothérapie  
Publication type: Peer-reviewed imaging and dosimetric study  
One-line takeaway: Gastrointestinal anatomy can change after online adaptation, altering estimated organ-at-risk exposure during abdominal SBRT.

Evidence tier: Small clinical imaging study of 20 lesions treated in five fractions; offline dosimetric assessment.

Importance score: 7/10

Key limitations:

- Small heterogeneous upper abdominal lesion cohort.
- Beginning and ending images do not capture continuous motion throughout delivery.
- No demonstrated relationship between measured changes and clinical toxicity.

Practical radiation oncology relevance:
Small bowel maximum dose increased by up to 1.5 Gy in the reported analysis. This matters near steep dose gradients and supports investigating verification imaging immediately before delivery, shorter adaptive sessions and explicit intrafraction action thresholds. It does not establish a universal margin or mandate further adaptation.

## Save for Later

### 4. The DE-RADIATE Study: Protocol for a Prospective, Non-Randomised Biomarker-Directed Phase II Study of 18F-FMISO PET-Guided Radical Radiation Dose De-Escalation in HPV-Associated Oropharyngeal Squamous Cell Carcinoma

Citation: Lawless AK, Bergamin S, Schembri G, Drummond J, Back M, Bailey DL, Brighi C, Brown C, Eade T, Guminski A, Lee A, Pang L, Parker L, Sarkis LM, Saurine T, Veivers D, Wignall A, Schoder H, Wray R, Lee N, Jayamanne D. BMC Cancer. Published November 17, 2026.[^4]

Journal/source: BMC Cancer  
Publication type: Peer-reviewed study protocol  
One-line takeaway: Intratreatment hypoxia PET is being tested as a selection tool for substantial RT dose reduction in selected HPV-associated disease.

Evidence tier: Planned single-arm phase II intervention; no efficacy results.

Importance score: 6/10

Key limitations:

- No outcome data in this publication.
- Single institution and no randomized control.
- Requires specialized hypoxia imaging and incorporates surgery, limiting extrapolation to definitive nonsurgical treatment.

Practical radiation oncology relevance:
Patients without detectable hypoxia on week-two FMISO PET are assigned 30 Gy in 15 fractions rather than 70 Gy, with platinum chemotherapy and subsequent nodal surgery for response assessment. The protocol also explores oxygen-enhanced MRI. It is a useful example of testing imaging-defined biology against pathological response, but the reduced prescription must remain investigational.

### 5. Brachytherapy Returns to the Brain: The ROADS Trial and a Second Look at Cavity Radiosurgery

Citation: Lehrer EJ, Brown PD. J Clin Oncol. 2026 Nov 16:JCO2602475. Online ahead of print.[^5]

Journal/source: Journal of Clinical Oncology  
Publication type: Editorial  
One-line takeaway: A new commentary revisits the role of intraoperative brachytherapy relative to postoperative stereotactic radiation.

Evidence tier: Expert commentary; no new primary data. Only partial editorial access was available.

Importance score: 5.5/10

Key limitations:

- Editorial rather than a new comparative study.
- Full arguments could not be independently assessed from the accessible text.
- The accompanying ROADS randomized trial was published September 28 and is outside this week's window.

Practical radiation oncology relevance:
For CNS teams considering intracranial brachytherapy, the relevant next step is reviewing the original ROADS trial and assessing implant logistics, surgical coordination, patient selection and comparative toxicity. The editorial should not be counted as independent confirmation of the trial.

## Low Priority

### 6. Development of a Statistical Framework for Optimizing Image-Guidance Frequency in Whole-Breast Radiotherapy

Citation: Tsuno H, Kosugi R, Sasaki K, Kiyohara H, Yoshimatsu Y, Kawashima Y. J Radiol Prot. 2026 Nov 18. Online ahead of print.[^6]

Journal/source: Journal of Radiological Protection  
Publication type: Peer-reviewed retrospective technical study  
One-line takeaway: Couch-coordinate deviations may provide a low-cost setup screening signal, but cannot yet safely replace scheduled image guidance.

Evidence tier: Retrospective analysis of 35 patients and 596 fractions.

Importance score: 4/10

Key limitations:

- Thresholds lack external validation and calibration to clinically meaningful dose endpoints.
- Couch coordinates cannot adequately represent all rotational or anatomical changes.
- Estimated imaging dose reductions are hypothetical, not measured patient benefits.

Practical radiation oncology relevance:
Vertical couch deviations correlated with image-guidance corrections (Spearman rho 0.75). This is an inexpensive candidate for local quality-improvement investigation, not a basis for reducing imaging frequency without prospective safety testing, particularly for short-course treatment.
