# Weekly Medical News and Clinical Horizon Scan: August 24–30, 2026

## Bottom Line This Week

The most clinically important trial was **SINGLE-AF**, which addressed a long-standing grey zone in atrial-fibrillation management. In patients with AF and intermediate stroke risk—CHA₂DS₂-VASc 1 in men or 2 in women—direct oral anticoagulation reduced the 24-month composite of stroke, systemic embolism, major bleeding, or cardiovascular death from 1.5% to 0.5%. The absolute benefit was modest, but this is randomized evidence in a population for whom recommendations have historically rested on weaker evidence.

The most important new therapy was **daraxonrasib (Rasonque)**, the first broad RAS-directed therapy approved for metastatic pancreatic adenocarcinoma. In a 500-patient randomized trial, median overall survival was 13.2 months versus 6.7 months with standard chemotherapy. That magnitude of survival benefit in previously treated metastatic pancreatic cancer is unusually large. The approval is FDA-only at present; Canadian authorization and reimbursement could not be verified.

The most important public-health development was the continuing **Bundibugyo Ebola outbreak in the Democratic Republic of the Congo**, which reached 5,794 confirmed DRC cases and 2,786 deaths by August 26 and remains a Public Health Emergency of International Concern. Canada extended temporary border measures through September 28, including 21-day quarantine for asymptomatic travellers arriving from affected areas and medical isolation for symptomatic travellers.

Other important developments included first-in-class or first-in-indication US approvals for **rusfertide in polycythemia vera** and **brepocitinib in adult dermatomyositis**, as well as FDA authorization of the first wearable device that continuously monitors both glucose and ketones.

The most credible emerging systems technology was **continuous dual glucose-ketone sensing**, which may allow earlier recognition of evolving diabetic ketoacidosis. By contrast, immediate evolocumab initiation during acute-MI PCI produced dramatic LDL lowering but no 12-month improvement in death or cardiovascular hospitalization.

Overall, this was a **clinically important week**. SINGLE-AF and daraxonrasib have credible claims to being practice-changing, while several other developments should affect specialist prescribing, device adoption, or public-health workflows soon.

## Must Know

### 1. Anticoagulation Benefits Patients With Atrial Fibrillation at Intermediate Stroke Risk

Citation: Kim D, Lee YS, Shim J, et al; SINGLE-AF Investigators. Anticoagulation for Atrial Fibrillation with Intermediate Stroke Risk.[^1]

Journal/source: New England Journal of Medicine

Publication type: Peer-reviewed multicentre randomized open-label adjudicator-masked superiority trial

One-line takeaway: DOAC therapy reduced net adverse clinical events in patients with AF and CHA₂DS₂-VASc scores of 1 in men or 2 in women.

Evidence tier: Randomized outcomes evidence directly addressing a guideline grey zone.

Evidence maturity: Potentially practice-changing.

Practice impact: Possibly soon.

Canadian relevance:
Direct oral anticoagulants are widely approved and reimbursed in Canada, but Canadian practice has generally individualized anticoagulation decisions at this intermediate-risk threshold. This trial may influence future Canadian Cardiovascular Society guidance and shared decision-making. The study population was South Korean, so absolute event rates may differ in Canadian populations.

Importance score: 10/10

Key limitations:

* The absolute event difference was only 1.0 percentage point over 24 months.
* The trial was open-label and conducted entirely in South Korea.
* Participants were relatively young, with a mean age of approximately 60 years.

Practical medical relevance:
Among 1,803 participants, the primary composite occurred in 0.5% with DOAC therapy versus 1.5% without anticoagulation; stroke occurred in 0.3% versus 1.1%. Major bleeding appeared similar between groups. The trial strengthens the case for anticoagulation in intermediate-risk AF, but the low absolute event rate means patient preferences and individual bleeding risk remain important.

### 2. Daraxonrasib Nearly Doubles Median Overall Survival in Previously Treated Metastatic Pancreatic Cancer

Citation: US Food and Drug Administration. FDA Approves First in Class Targeted Therapy for Metastatic Pancreatic Cancer.[^2]

Journal/source: US Food and Drug Administration; phase III randomized trial

Publication type: Regulatory approval supported by randomized open-label phase III evidence

One-line takeaway: The broad RAS inhibitor daraxonrasib improved median overall survival from 6.7 to 13.2 months versus standard chemotherapy in previously treated metastatic pancreatic adenocarcinoma.

Evidence tier: Randomized phase III survival evidence with a large treatment effect.

Evidence maturity: Ready for US practice.

Practice impact: Now in the United States; possibly soon elsewhere.

Canadian relevance:
Health Canada approval and Canadian reimbursement could not be verified as of August 30, 2026. This FDA approval does not immediately change Canadian practice. If submitted in Canada, uptake would depend on regulatory review, provincial oncology funding, toxicity management, and treatment sequencing.

Importance score: 10/10

Key limitations:

* The FDA announcement provides less detail than a full peer-reviewed trial report.
* The trial was in previously treated disease or patients unsuitable for multiagent systemic therapy.
* Rash, diarrhea, stomatitis, gastrointestinal toxicity, edema, appetite loss, and hemorrhage were common adverse effects.

Practical medical relevance:
Pancreatic adenocarcinoma remains one of the most lethal common malignancies, and RAS has historically been difficult to drug. A survival improvement of roughly 6.5 months in metastatic disease is clinically meaningful and unusually large for this setting.

### 3. Ebola Outbreak Expands Further as Canada Extends Border Measures

Citation: World Health Organization and Public Health Agency of Canada. Bundibugyo Ebola outbreak update and extension of temporary Canadian border measures.[^3]

Journal/source: World Health Organization; Public Health Agency of Canada

Publication type: Public-health emergency update and national border-health policy

One-line takeaway: The DRC outbreak reached 5,794 confirmed cases and 2,786 deaths by August 26, while Canada extended quarantine and screening measures through September 28.

Evidence tier: Official international and Canadian public-health surveillance and policy.

Evidence maturity: Ready for public-health action.

Practice impact: Now.

Canadian relevance:
This directly applies to Canada. Travellers entering Canada after being in affected areas within the prior 21 days who have symptoms consistent with Ebola are to be isolated at a medical facility; asymptomatic travellers undergo health assessment and are required to quarantine for 21 days. No Canadian cases had been reported as of August 28. Canada has authorized an early-phase candidate Bundibugyo vaccine trial, but there remains no licensed vaccine or proven virus-specific therapy for this Ebola species.

Importance score: 10/10

Key limitations:

* Case counts continue to evolve rapidly.
* Some increase reflects improved surveillance and testing as well as true geographic expansion.
* Border measures reduce risk but cannot guarantee prevention of importation.

Practical medical relevance:
The outbreak now affects 60 health zones across six DRC provinces and has a crude case-fatality ratio of approximately 48%. For Canadian physicians, the practical issue is immediate recognition of epidemiologic risk, isolation of a compatible symptomatic patient, and urgent public-health notification.

## Important Developments

### 4. Rusfertide Becomes the First Hepcidin-Mimetic Therapy Approved for Polycythemia Vera

Citation: US Food and Drug Administration. FDA Approves First Drug of Its Kind for Polycythemia Vera, a Rare Blood Disorder.[^4]

Journal/source: US Food and Drug Administration

Publication type: Regulatory approval supported by a randomized double-blind placebo-controlled phase III trial

One-line takeaway: Weekly rusfertide markedly reduced phlebotomy requirements in patients with polycythemia vera inadequately controlled with standard therapy.

Evidence tier: Phase III randomized evidence using clinically relevant hematocrit and phlebotomy outcomes.

Evidence maturity: Ready for US practice.

Practice impact: Now in the United States.

Canadian relevance:
Health Canada approval and Canadian reimbursement could not be verified as of August 30, 2026. Canadian hematology practice should therefore not change solely on the basis of the FDA decision.

Importance score: 8/10

Key limitations:

* The pivotal endpoint focused on avoidance of phlebotomy rather than thrombosis, cardiovascular events, or survival.
* Follow-up was 32 weeks for the primary placebo-controlled phase.
* Anemia and injection-site reactions remain clinically relevant adverse effects.

Practical medical relevance:
In VERIFY, 76.9% of rusfertide-treated patients required no phlebotomies during the study period versus 32.9% with placebo. Mimicking hepcidin offers a different strategy: restricting iron availability to erythropoiesis rather than suppressing marrow proliferation directly.

### 5. Brepocitinib Becomes the First FDA-Approved Oral Treatment for Adult Dermatomyositis

Citation: US Food and Drug Administration. FDA Approves First Oral Drug Indicated to Treat Dermatomyositis in Adults.[^5]

Journal/source: US Food and Drug Administration

Publication type: Regulatory approval supported by a phase III randomized double-blind placebo-controlled trial

One-line takeaway: Once-daily brepocitinib improved global dermatomyositis disease activity and facilitated corticosteroid reduction over 52 weeks.

Evidence tier: Phase III randomized clinical evidence.

Evidence maturity: Ready for US practice.

Practice impact: Now in the United States.

Canadian relevance:
Health Canada authorization and Canadian reimbursement could not be verified as of August 30, 2026. If authorized, uptake would depend on specialist selection, infection screening, cardiovascular and thrombotic risk assessment, and reimbursement.

Importance score: 8/10

Key limitations:

* The primary endpoint was a composite Total Improvement Score rather than mortality or irreversible disability.
* Long-term safety remains important for chronic JAK-pathway inhibition.
* The drug carries a boxed warning for serious infection, malignancy, major cardiovascular events, thrombosis, and increased mortality.

Practical medical relevance:
Dermatomyositis treatment often relies on corticosteroids and off-label immunosuppressants. A specifically approved oral therapy provides a standardized alternative and may reduce steroid burden, but clinicians should not interpret an oral formulation as low risk.

### 6. Continuous Ketone and Glucose Monitoring Enters Clinical Practice

Citation: US Food and Drug Administration. FDA Authorizes First Wearable Device That Continuously Monitors Both Ketone Levels and Blood Sugar.[^6]

Journal/source: US Food and Drug Administration

Publication type: De Novo medical-device marketing authorization

One-line takeaway: Abbott's Libre Duo 10 Day became the first wearable device authorized to continuously measure both glucose and ketone levels.

Evidence tier: Regulatory clinical-performance evidence from six studies involving more than 600 participants.

Evidence maturity: Ready for selected US clinical use.

Practice impact: Possibly soon.

Canadian relevance:
Health Canada authorization and Canadian availability could not be verified as of August 30, 2026. Canadian adoption would depend on device licensing, coverage, integration with diabetes programs, and whether continuous ketone information improves meaningful outcomes such as DKA admissions.

Importance score: 8/10

Key limitations:

* Authorization is based primarily on analytical and clinical performance, not randomized evidence that the device reduces DKA or hospitalization.
* Ketone values still require interpretation alongside glucose levels and clinical symptoms.
* Cost, alarm burden, adherence, and usefulness outside high-risk groups remain uncertain.

Practical medical relevance:
The device measures both analytes every minute and can alert users to rising ketone concentrations before overt DKA. The technology may be especially useful for type 1 diabetes, intercurrent illness, insulin-pump failure, and possibly SGLT2-associated ketosis, but outcome studies are still needed.

### 7. TTR Gene Silencing Reduces Mortality and Cardiovascular Events Across Two ATTR-CM Outcomes Trials

Citation: Gillmore JD, Hamatani Y, Fontana M, et al. Gene Silencer Therapy in Transthyretin Amyloid Cardiomyopathy: A Meta-Analysis of Outcomes Trials.[^7]

Journal/source: JAMA

Publication type: Prespecified meta-analysis of two randomized placebo-controlled phase III cardiovascular outcomes trials

One-line takeaway: Across HELIOS-B and CARDIO-TTRansform, TTR gene-silencing therapies reduced all-cause death and recurrent cardiovascular events despite one constituent trial missing its primary endpoint.

Evidence tier: Meta-analysis of randomized outcomes trials using harmonized clinical endpoints.

Evidence maturity: Potentially practice-changing.

Practice impact: Possibly soon.

Canadian relevance:
Specific TTR-silencing therapies have differing Health Canada indications and provincial coverage; regulatory and reimbursement status should be checked product by product. The pooled result supports the clinical validity of TTR suppression but does not imply interchangeability of all agents.

Importance score: 8/10

Key limitations:

* Only two pivotal trials were available.
* Trial populations, background stabilizer use, endpoint definitions, and follow-up differed.
* Some analyses used individual participant data that were not fully published before the meta-analysis.

Practical medical relevance:
Gene silencers reduced the composite of all-cause death and recurrent cardiovascular events by approximately 20% and all-cause mortality by about 26%. The result is important because it suggests a class-level clinical effect despite discordant individual trial results.

### 8. Metformin Does Not Prevent Gestational Diabetes in High-Metabolic-Risk Pregnancies

Citation: Mousa A, Løvvik TS, Carlsen SM, et al. Metformin in High Metabolic Risk Pregnancies — A Patient-Level Meta-Analysis.[^8]

Journal/source: NEJM Evidence

Publication type: Individual-participant-data meta-analysis of randomized placebo-controlled trials

One-line takeaway: Preventive metformin did not reduce gestational diabetes overall, although it modestly prolonged gestation and was associated with fewer preterm births.

Evidence tier: High-quality patient-level synthesis of randomized trials.

Evidence maturity: Ready to inform practice.

Practice impact: Now.

Canadian relevance:
Metformin is widely available in Canada, but this analysis does not support routine prophylactic metformin solely to prevent gestational diabetes in high-risk pregnant patients without diabetes. Canadian obstetric and diabetes guidance should remain the basis for treatment decisions.

Importance score: 7/10

Key limitations:

* Only 7 of 10 eligible trials supplied individual-level data.
* Results varied somewhat according to the diagnostic definition of gestational diabetes.
* Gastrointestinal adverse effects were more common with metformin.

Practical medical relevance:
Across 2,297 pregnancies, metformin did not reduce GDM under the most commonly used criteria. The lower odds of preterm birth are intriguing but should be considered secondary to the neutral primary prevention result rather than a reason to prescribe metformin routinely.

Citation: Health Canada. Health Product InfoWatch, August 2026.[^9]

Journal/source: Health Canada MedEffect Canada

Publication type: National pharmacovigilance and health-product safety update

One-line takeaway: Health Canada published its August postmarket safety summary, reinforcing the need to distinguish regulatory approval from ongoing real-world safety assessment.

Evidence tier: Official pharmacovigilance surveillance.

Evidence maturity: Ready for practice.

Practice impact: Now.

Canadian relevance:
This is directly Canadian. Health Product InfoWatch consolidates emerging adverse-event signals, product-information changes, safety communications, and regulatory monitoring. Clinicians should use it alongside product monographs, recalls, and specialty guidance rather than assuming approval represents a static safety determination.

Importance score: 6/10

Key limitations:

* Spontaneous adverse-event reporting cannot establish incidence or causality reliably.
* Safety signals may be preliminary.
* The monthly summary aggregates heterogeneous products and evidence types.

Practical medical relevance:
For rapidly evolving therapies—from JAK inhibitors to gene therapies and novel oncology agents—postmarket surveillance often identifies rare harms that pivotal trials are too small or too short to detect.

## On the Horizon

### 10. More Extensive AF Ablation Is Not Uniformly Better

Citation: Roten L, Maurhofer J, Krisai P, et al. Pulmonary Vein Isolation Using Pulsed Field Ablation With vs Without Posterior Wall Isolation in Patients With Symptomatic Persistent Atrial Fibrillation: The PIFPAF-PFA Randomized Clinical Trial.[^10]

Journal/source: JAMA

Publication type: Peer-reviewed randomized clinical trial

One-line takeaway: Adding posterior-wall isolation to pulsed-field pulmonary-vein isolation did not significantly reduce recurrent atrial tachyarrhythmia in persistent AF.

Evidence tier: Randomized procedural evidence.

Evidence maturity: Ready to inform procedural strategy but awaiting integration with parallel ablation trials.

Practice impact: Possibly soon.

Canadian relevance:
Pulsed-field ablation is increasingly used in Canadian electrophysiology centres, but local device availability and program protocols vary. This study suggests that empiric additional posterior-wall ablation should not be assumed to improve outcomes.

Importance score: 7/10

Key limitations:

* Only 206 patients were randomized.
* Recurrence was numerically lower with posterior-wall isolation but did not reach statistical significance.
* Findings should be interpreted alongside other persistent-AF substrate-ablation trials.

Practical medical relevance:
Atrial tachyarrhythmia recurred in 50.6% with posterior-wall isolation versus 60.6% with pulmonary-vein isolation alone. The result argues against routine anatomical escalation without clear evidence.

## Hype Check

### 11. Immediate Evolocumab After Acute MI Produces Spectacular LDL Lowering but No 12-Month Clinical Benefit

Citation: Montalescot G, Ferrari E, Souteyrand G, et al. LDL Cholesterol Lowering With Evolocumab Before Percutaneous Coronary Intervention for Acute Myocardial Infarction: The AMUNDSEN Randomized Clinical Trial.[^11]

Journal/source: JAMA

Publication type: Peer-reviewed randomized open-label blinded-endpoint clinical trial

One-line takeaway: Starting evolocumab in the catheterization laboratory dramatically improved LDL target attainment but did not reduce death or unplanned cardiovascular hospitalization at 12 months.

Evidence tier: Large randomized trial with a surrogate primary endpoint and neutral prespecified clinical endpoint.

Evidence maturity: Ready to inform lipid-lowering strategy; clinical-outcome benefit from immediate initiation remains unproven.

Practice impact: Possibly soon for LDL implementation, not yet for outcome-based superiority.

Canadian relevance:
Evolocumab is approved in Canada, but reimbursement criteria vary by province and payer. This trial does not establish that giving a PCSK9 inhibitor before PCI improves short-term clinical outcomes compared with rapid guideline-directed oral lipid lowering and later escalation.

Importance score: 7/10

Key limitations:

* The primary endpoint was LDL target attainment rather than cardiovascular events.
* The 12-month follow-up may be too short to capture benefits of sustained LDL reduction.
* Cost and reimbursement substantially affect real-world feasibility.

Practical medical relevance:
At 12 months, 81.6% of patients assigned to immediate evolocumab achieved both at least 50% LDL reduction and LDL below 55 mg/dL versus 39.6% with standard care. Yet death or unplanned cardiovascular hospitalization occurred in 14.6% versus 15.4%, respectively.

## Weekly Takeaways

**Most likely to change practice:** SINGLE-AF. It provides randomized evidence supporting anticoagulation in a population where guidelines have historically relied on observational risk estimates and expert consensus.

**Most important new therapeutic development:** Daraxonrasib for metastatic pancreatic adenocarcinoma, because the reported overall-survival improvement is large and based on randomized comparative evidence.

**Most important safety or public-health update:** The expanding Bundibugyo Ebola outbreak and Canada's extension of 21-day quarantine and border-health measures through September 28.

**Most interesting emerging technology:** Continuous dual glucose-ketone monitoring, which may convert ketone surveillance from intermittent testing into an early-warning system for evolving DKA.

**Most overhyped story:** Immediate catheterization-lab evolocumab as a clinical-outcome breakthrough. LDL lowering was dramatic; one-year clinical events were unchanged.

**One development physicians may be underestimating:** TTR gene silencing. Pooling two major outcomes trials suggests a class-level reduction in mortality and cardiovascular events even when individual trial results look discordant.

**What patients may ask about this week:** Whether everyone with AF and one stroke-risk factor now needs anticoagulation, whether the new pancreatic-cancer drug is available in Canada, whether continuous ketone monitors can prevent DKA, and whether travellers returning from central Africa need Ebola quarantine.
