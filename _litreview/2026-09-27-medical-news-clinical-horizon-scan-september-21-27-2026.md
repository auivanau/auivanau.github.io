# Weekly Medical News and Clinical Horizon Scan: September 21--27, 2026

## Bottom Line This Week

The most broadly consequential therapeutic development was FDA approval
of **once-weekly insulin efsitora alfa (Onswik) for adults with type 2
diabetes**. Across the phase III QWINT program, weekly efsitora achieved
glycemic control broadly comparable with daily basal insulin while
reducing basal injections from 365 to 52 per year. This is primarily an
adherence and treatment-burden innovation rather than a superior
glucose-lowering therapy, and it is not recommended for type 1 diabetes
because of severe-hypoglycemia risk.

The week's other major general-neurology approval was **tavapadon
(Juvmo)**, a once-daily selective dopamine D1/D5 partial agonist for
Parkinson disease. Phase III studies support monotherapy in earlier
disease and adjunctive use with levodopa in motor fluctuations. It
represents a genuinely new oral dopaminergic mechanism, although
long-term comparative tolerability remains uncertain.

FDA also approved **obinutuzumab for frequently relapsing or
steroid-dependent childhood-onset idiopathic nephrotic syndrome** in
patients aged 2 years or older who are in remission. In INShore, 95%
receiving obinutuzumab versus 73% receiving mycophenolate mofetil were
relapse-free after week 8 and in complete remission at one year.

In pulmonary vascular medicine, HYPERION data added to the sotatercept
label showed a 76% relative reduction in clinical worsening among adults
with recently diagnosed PAH. The indication itself is unchanged, but the
result strengthens the argument for introducing sotatercept earlier.

The most important public-health development remains the **Bundibugyo
Ebola epidemic in the Democratic Republic of the Congo**. By September
23, WHO reported 7,890 confirmed cases and 3,799 deaths across 63 health
zones in seven provinces.

The most discussed diagnostic technology was **PATHFINDER 2 multicancer
early detection**. Specificity was 99.64% and positive predictive value
60.3%, but overall episode sensitivity was only 39.3%. Most importantly,
the study did not test whether screening reduces cancer mortality.

Overall, this was a **clinically important regulatory week rather than
one dominated by new practice-changing randomized trials**. Weekly
insulin and tavapadon may alter chronic disease management; obinutuzumab
meaningfully changes a rare pediatric renal disease; and earlier
sotatercept use in PAH is supported by strong clinical-worsening data.
Multicancer blood testing remains the clearest example of promising
technology outrunning proof of patient benefit.

## Must Know

### 1. Once-Weekly Insulin Efsitora Alfa Is Approved for Type 2 Diabetes

Citation: US Food and Drug Administration. FDA Approves Once-Weekly
Insulin for Type 2 Diabetes Mellitus.[^1]

Journal/source: US Food and Drug Administration; QWINT phase III program

Publication type: Regulatory approval supported by multiple phase III
randomized trials

One-line takeaway: Insulin efsitora alfa provides once-weekly basal
insulin with glycemic efficacy broadly noninferior to daily basal
insulin in adults with type 2 diabetes.

Evidence tier: Large phase III clinical-development program with
established glycemic endpoints.

Evidence maturity: Ready for practice in the United States.

Practice impact: Now.

Canadian relevance: Health Canada authorization could not be verified as
of September 27, 2026. If authorized, value will depend on cost,
adherence, patient preference, safe transition protocols, and provincial
coverage.

Importance score: 9/10

Key limitations:

-   The principal advantage is reduced injection frequency, not superior
    glycemic efficacy or proven cardiovascular benefit.
-   Weekly pharmacokinetics make dosing errors potentially more
    consequential.
-   FDA does not recommend efsitora for type 1 diabetes because of
    increased severe-hypoglycemia risk.

Practical medical relevance: A basal insulin given 52 rather than 365
times per year could reduce treatment burden and resistance to insulin
initiation. More than 3,400 adults were studied across QWINT, with
noninferior A1C reduction versus glargine or degludec. The key question
is whether convenience produces meaningful real-world adherence gains.

### 2. Tavapadon Introduces a New Oral Dopaminergic Mechanism for Parkinson Disease

Citation: US Food and Drug Administration. Juvmo (tavapadon) approval
for Parkinson disease in adults.[^2]

Journal/source: US Food and Drug Administration; TEMPO phase III program

Publication type: Regulatory approval supported by three phase III
randomized placebo-controlled trials

One-line takeaway: Tavapadon became the first approved selective D1/D5
dopamine-receptor partial agonist for Parkinson disease.

Evidence tier: Multiple phase III randomized trials demonstrating
symptomatic motor benefit.

Evidence maturity: Ready for practice in the United States.

Practice impact: Now.

Canadian relevance: Health Canada approval and Canadian reimbursement
could not be verified as of September 27, 2026. Its eventual role will
depend on pricing and whether D1/D5 selectivity yields a meaningful
tolerability advantage over existing dopaminergic therapy.

Importance score: 9/10

Key limitations:

-   Trials establish symptomatic benefit, not neuroprotection.
-   Nausea, dizziness, headache, hallucinations, and discontinuation
    remain relevant.
-   Long-term comparative effectiveness against levodopa and older
    dopamine agonists is unclear.

Practical medical relevance: TEMPO-1 and TEMPO-2 improved MDS-UPDRS
Parts II and III in early disease. In TEMPO-3, adjunctive tavapadon
increased daily "on" time without troublesome dyskinesia by about 1.1
hours versus placebo. D1/D5 selectivity is pharmacologically novel;
real-world tolerability will determine whether it is clinically
distinctive.

### 3. Obinutuzumab Becomes the First FDA-Approved Idiopathic Nephrotic Syndrome Therapy in Decades

Citation: US Food and Drug Administration. FDA Approves Drug to Treat
Idiopathic Nephrotic Syndrome in Patients 2 Years and Older.[^3]

Journal/source: US Food and Drug Administration; INShore phase III trial

Publication type: Regulatory approval supported by a phase III
active-comparator trial

One-line takeaway: Obinutuzumab substantially reduced relapse versus
mycophenolate mofetil in frequently relapsing or steroid-dependent
childhood-onset idiopathic nephrotic syndrome.

Evidence tier: Phase III comparative evidence with a clinically
meaningful relapse endpoint.

Evidence maturity: Ready for practice in the United States.

Practice impact: Now.

Canadian relevance: Obinutuzumab is available in Canada for other
indications, but Health Canada authorization for idiopathic nephrotic
syndrome could not be verified as of September 27, 2026.

Importance score: 9/10

Key limitations:

-   The population was restricted to childhood-onset disease in complete
    remission.
-   Anti-CD20 therapy carries infusion, infection,
    hepatitis-B-reactivation, and hypogammaglobulinemia risks.
-   Longer follow-up is needed to define durability and retreatment.

Practical medical relevance: In INShore, 95% receiving obinutuzumab
versus 73% receiving mycophenolate had no relapse after week 8 and
remained in complete remission at week 52. For children repeatedly
exposed to corticosteroids and chronic immunosuppression, durable B-cell
depletion could substantially change cumulative treatment burden.

## Important Developments

### 4. HYPERION Strengthens the Case for Earlier Sotatercept in Pulmonary Arterial Hypertension

Citation: HYPERION investigators; US Food and Drug Administration.
Sotatercept-csrk label update incorporating HYPERION phase III data.[^4]

Journal/source: FDA label update; phase III HYPERION trial

Publication type: Randomized phase III trial and regulatory label update

One-line takeaway: Adding sotatercept to background therapy in recently
diagnosed intermediate- or high-risk PAH reduced clinical worsening by
76% versus placebo.

Evidence tier: High-quality randomized clinical-outcome evidence.

Evidence maturity: Ready for practice within the existing US indication.

Practice impact: Now.

Canadian relevance: Sotatercept is available in Canada for selected
adults with WHO Group 1 PAH; reimbursement is jurisdiction-specific.
HYPERION supports earlier use, but Canadian guideline and payer criteria
may lag emerging evidence.

Importance score: 9/10

Key limitations:

-   The trial stopped early after separate ZENITH results altered
    equipoise.
-   Sotatercept remains an add-on rather than replacement for background
    PAH therapy.
-   Erythrocytosis, thrombocytopenia, bleeding, telangiectasia, and
    blood-count monitoring matter.

Practical medical relevance: Clinical worsening occurred in 10.6% with
sotatercept versus 36.9% with placebo (HR 0.24). The magnitude
strengthens a broader move toward earlier multidrug PAH therapy.

### 5. Belzutifan Plus Lenvatinib Gains FDA Approval After PD-1/PD-L1 Therapy in Advanced Clear-Cell RCC

Citation: US Food and Drug Administration. FDA approves belzutifan in
combination with lenvatinib for advanced renal cell carcinoma with a
clear cell component.[^5]

Journal/source: US Food and Drug Administration; LITESPARK-011

Publication type: Regulatory approval supported by a randomized
active-controlled phase III trial

One-line takeaway: FDA approved belzutifan plus lenvatinib after prior
PD-1/PD-L1 therapy based on randomized evidence against cabozantinib.

Evidence tier: Large randomized phase III comparative evidence.

Evidence maturity: Ready for US practice.

Practice impact: Now.

Canadian relevance: Health Canada approval and provincial reimbursement
for this specific combination and sequence could not be verified as of
September 27, 2026.

Importance score: 8/10

Key limitations:

-   The combination carries substantial toxicity and monitoring burden.
-   Belzutifan can cause severe anemia and hypoxia; lenvatinib adds
    cardiovascular, renal, hepatic, and gastrointestinal risks.
-   Optimal post-immunotherapy sequencing remains unsettled.

Practical medical relevance: LITESPARK-011 randomized 747 patients
against an established active comparator, making the evidence more
clinically informative than a single-arm approval and further
establishing HIF-2α inhibition in clear-cell RCC.

### 6. Lirafugratinib Adds a Selective FGFR2 Option for Cholangiocarcinoma

Citation: US Food and Drug Administration. FDA approves lirafugratinib
for previously treated, unresectable, locally advanced or metastatic
cholangiocarcinoma.[^6]

Journal/source: US Food and Drug Administration; REFOCUS

Publication type: Regulatory approval based on a multicentre single-arm
trial

One-line takeaway: Lirafugratinib produced a 46% response rate with
median response duration of 11.8 months in FGFR2-fusion/rearranged
cholangiocarcinoma.

Evidence tier: Strong single-arm targeted-therapy signal in a
biomarker-defined rare population.

Evidence maturity: Ready for selected US patients.

Practice impact: Now.

Canadian relevance: Health Canada approval and reimbursement could not
be verified. The development reinforces comprehensive molecular
profiling in advanced cholangiocarcinoma.

Importance score: 7/10

Key limitations:

-   REFOCUS was single-arm and enrolled 116 patients.
-   Approval rests on response rate and duration rather than randomized
    survival.
-   Ocular toxicity, hyperphosphatemia, and soft-tissue mineralization
    require monitoring.

Practical medical relevance: A 46% response rate and 11.8-month median
response duration are clinically meaningful in this setting. Cross-trial
comparisons with existing FGFR inhibitors remain unreliable.

### 7. Long-Term Gene-Therapy Data in Wiskott--Aldrich Syndrome Remain Strong

Citation: Ferrua F, Cenciarelli S, et al. Etuvetidigene Autotemcel for
the Treatment of Wiskott--Aldrich Syndrome.[^7]

Journal/source: New England Journal of Medicine

Publication type: Integrated prospective open-label phase I--III and
expanded-access analysis

One-line takeaway: Autologous lentiviral hematopoietic stem-cell gene
therapy produced 96% five-year survival with sustained reductions in
severe infection and bleeding.

Evidence tier: Small but unusually mature rare-disease gene-therapy
evidence.

Evidence maturity: Ready for practice where approved and accessible.

Practice impact: Now in jurisdictions where available.

Canadian relevance: The therapy is approved in the United States and
European Union; Health Canada authorization and reimbursement could not
be verified.

Importance score: 8/10

Key limitations:

-   Only 27 patients contributed to the analysis.
-   There was no randomized transplant comparator.
-   Conditioning, individualized manufacturing, cost, and specialized
    delivery remain substantial barriers.

Practical medical relevance: At median 5.7-year follow-up, survival was
96%. Severe infections fell from 2.00 to 0.15 events per person-year and
moderate/severe bleeding from 2.00 to 0.80. No insertional oncogenesis
was observed, making the long follow-up particularly valuable.

## Safety, Regulation, and Public Health

### 8. Bundibugyo Ebola Expands to Seven Provinces in the Democratic Republic of the Congo

Citation: World Health Organization. Ebola disease caused by Bundibugyo
virus --- Democratic Republic of the Congo. Disease Outbreak News,
September 25, 2026.[^8]

Journal/source: World Health Organization

Publication type: Public-health outbreak surveillance and emergency
update

One-line takeaway: The epidemic reached 7,890 confirmed cases and 3,799
deaths by September 23 and expanded into additional health zones and a
new province.

Evidence tier: Official national and WHO outbreak surveillance.

Evidence maturity: Actionable public-health information.

Practice impact: Now.

Canadian relevance: Risk to the Canadian general population remains low,
but widening geographic spread increases the importance of travel and
exposure histories. Suspected cases require immediate isolation,
infection-prevention precautions, and urgent public-health consultation.

Importance score: 9/10

Key limitations:

-   Counts are dynamic and subject to reconciliation.
-   Conflict and health-system disruption impair surveillance and care.
-   National totals obscure major geographic heterogeneity.

Practical medical relevance: Since WHO's September 10 update, 1,133
additional confirmed cases and 532 deaths were reported. Sixty-three
health zones across seven provinces had been affected, with spread
toward international borders. The crude case-fatality ratio remained
48.1%.

## On the Horizon

### 9. Anito-cel Shows Durable Activity With Limited Severe Neurotoxicity in Refractory Myeloma

Citation: Frigault MJ, Dhakal B, Jakubowiak AJ, et al. Phase 1 Study of
Anito-cel, a d-Domain BCMA CAR T Cell for Refractory or Recurrent
Myeloma.[^9]

Journal/source: New England Journal of Medicine

Publication type: Phase I single-arm cellular-therapy trial with
translational mechanistic studies

One-line takeaway: All 38 treated patients responded, 79% achieved
complete response, and median PFS was 30.2 months, with little
high-grade CRS or ICANS and no observed delayed non-ICANS neurotoxicity.

Evidence tier: Very strong early efficacy signal but small single-arm
phase I evidence.

Evidence maturity: Early clinical evidence.

Practice impact: Possibly soon if later-phase evidence confirms the
profile.

Canadian relevance: Anito-cel is investigational and not Health Canada
approved. Existing Canadian CAR-T capacity, manufacturing, referral, and
reimbursement constraints would also apply.

Importance score: 8/10

Key limitations:

-   Only 38 patients were treated without a comparator.
-   Every patient had an adverse event and 97% had a grade 3 or higher
    event.
-   Cross-trial neurotoxicity comparisons are unreliable.

Practical medical relevance: At 38.1 months' median follow-up, response
was 100%, complete response 79%, median PFS 30.2 months, and 36-month
survival 65%. If the favorable delayed-neurotoxicity profile persists,
CAR-binder engineering may become an important differentiator.

### 10. Zilurgisertib Adds Targeted ALK2 Inhibition for Fibrodysplasia Ossificans Progressiva

Citation: US Food and Drug Administration. FDA Approves Third Treatment
for Fibrodysplasia Ossificans Progressiva.[^10]

Journal/source: US Food and Drug Administration; PROGRESS phase II trial

Publication type: Regulatory approval supported by a randomized phase II
placebo-controlled trial

One-line takeaway: Zilurgisertib reduced new heterotopic-ossification
volume in patients aged 12 years or older with FOP.

Evidence tier: Randomized phase II imaging-endpoint evidence in an
ultra-rare genetic disease.

Evidence maturity: Ready for US practice but clinically important
uncertainties remain.

Practice impact: Now in the United States.

Canadian relevance: Health Canada authorization could not be verified.
Any Canadian use would require specialized rare-disease access and
reimbursement.

Importance score: 7/10

Key limitations:

-   Only 63 patients were randomized.
-   Approval is based on CT-measured heterotopic-ossification volume
    rather than function or survival.
-   Comparative effectiveness among the now multiple FOP therapies is
    unknown.

Practical medical relevance: At week 24, mean total new
heterotopic-ossification volume decreased by 3.2 cm³ with zilurgisertib
versus a 24.6 cm³ increase with placebo. The key unanswered question is
whether imaging suppression preserves long-term function.

## Hype Check

### 11. PATHFINDER 2 Makes Multicancer Blood Testing More Credible---but Still Does Not Prove Screening Benefit

Citation: PATHFINDER 2 investigators. Performance and safety of a
multi-cancer early detection test: the PATHFINDER 2 study.[^11]

Journal/source: Nature Medicine

Publication type: Large prospective interventional diagnostic study

One-line takeaway: A multicancer cfDNA test achieved 99.64% specificity
and 60.3% positive predictive value, but detected only 39.3% of all
cancer episodes and has not been shown to reduce cancer mortality.

Evidence tier: Strong prospective diagnostic-performance evidence
without randomized clinical-outcome evidence.

Evidence maturity: Awaiting randomized evidence of clinical benefit.

Practice impact: Unlikely to change routine population screening yet.

Canadian relevance: No Canadian population-screening recommendation
supports routine MCED testing, and Health Canada authorization or
provincial reimbursement for this specific assay could not be verified.
It should not replace established breast, cervical, colorectal, or lung
screening.

Importance score: 8/10

Key limitations:

-   The study was descriptive and performed no hypothesis testing.
-   Overall episode sensitivity was only 39.3%.
-   No evidence shows that MCED screening reduces cancer mortality or
    improves quality of life.

Practical medical relevance: Among 32,007 performance-analyzable adults,
cancer detection rate was 0.54%, PPV 60.3%, NPV 99.2%, specificity
99.64%, and the number needed to screen to detect one cancer was 185.
Screening requires more than accuracy: stage shift, overdiagnosis,
downstream procedures, cost, and mortality remain decisive.

## Weekly Takeaways

**Most likely to change practice:** Once-weekly basal insulin. The
innovation is not superior glucose lowering but dramatically lower
injection burden.

**Most important new therapeutic development:** Tavapadon. Selective
D1/D5 partial agonism creates a genuinely new oral pharmacologic option
in Parkinson disease.

**Most important safety or public-health update:** Bundibugyo Ebola, now
geographically broader with a crude case-fatality ratio near 48%.

**Most interesting emerging technology:** Anito-cel. Durable myeloma
activity and a potentially favorable neurotoxicity profile suggest
CAR-binder engineering may alter therapeutic index.

**Most overhyped story:** Multicancer early-detection blood testing.
PATHFINDER 2 improves confidence in diagnostic performance but does not
show that screening saves lives.

**One development physicians may be underestimating:** Obinutuzumab in
childhood-onset idiopathic nephrotic syndrome. A 22-percentage-point
absolute difference in one-year relapse-free complete remission could
materially reduce steroid and immunosuppressive exposure.

**What patients may ask about this week:** Whether insulin can really be
taken once a week, whether the new Parkinson pill is better than
levodopa, whether one blood test can screen for many cancers, and
whether the Ebola outbreak poses a risk to Canada.
