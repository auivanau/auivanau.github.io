# Weekly Medical News and Clinical Horizon Scan: October 26–November 1, 2026

## Bottom Line This Week

**The most broadly consequential new clinical trial is LEAP-Block**, a randomized comparison of left bundle-branch area pacing (LBBAP) versus conventional right-ventricular pacing in patients with atrioventricular block, preserved left-ventricular ejection fraction, and an anticipated high pacing burden. At 24 months, the adjusted composite of death, heart-failure events, or upgrade to biventricular pacing was 2.4% versus 12.5%; the difference was predominantly driven by heart-failure events rather than demonstrated mortality reduction. This is potentially practice-changing for electrophysiology programs, although operator expertise and long-term lead durability matter.

**The most important new cancer treatment evidence is TroFuse-005**, published in *The Lancet* on October 26. In advanced or recurrent endometrial cancer after platinum chemotherapy and immunotherapy, the TROP2 antibody–drug conjugate (ADC) sacituzumab tirumotecan improved median overall survival to 19.0 months versus 12.1 months with physician's-choice chemotherapy (hazard ratio [HR] 0.68). This is a genuine survival outcome, not just a response-rate signal, but the drug remains investigational in Canada and the United States as of this digest.

**A consequential fertility trial challenges the assumption that several cycles of stimulated intrauterine insemination (IUI) are equivalent to first-line IVF.** In FIIX, one complete IVF cycle yielded a 55% early cumulative live-birth rate versus 31% after up to four stimulated IUI cycles. After access to further IVF, however, cumulative live-birth rates converged to approximately 70% in both strategies. The decision is therefore as much about *time, cost, access, and treatment burden* as ultimate probability of a live birth.

The most important **new US approval for a common malignancy** is talazoparib plus enzalutamide for homologous-recombination-repair (HRR)–mutated metastatic hormone-sensitive/androgen-pathway-modulation–naïve prostate cancer. TALAPRO-3 showed a radiographic progression-free-survival HR of 0.48, but overall survival is immature. **Health Canada participated in the parallel Project Orbis review, which remained ongoing** when the FDA approved the indication on October 26; the US decision must not be treated as Canadian authorization.

The **most important Canadian public-health update** is the October 27 Chief Public Health Officer's annual report: life expectancy is approaching its pre-pandemic level, but healthy years have not recovered in parallel, and syphilis, other sexually transmitted infections, mental-health inequities, wildfire exposure, and toxic-drug harms remain serious concerns. An October 29 Health Canada recall also warns that specified **Vitros HbA1c slides may produce falsely high results**, potentially prompting inappropriate diabetes treatment changes.

The **most urgent global public-health signal** is the worsening Bundibugyo Ebola epidemic. October 30–31 reporting based on Congolese government data put the DRC toll above 10,000 cases and 5,000 deaths; WHO's latest fully detailed outbreak bulletin located during this review was dated October 23 and documented international spread to Kenya and Nigeria. These are different reporting dates and should not be combined as if they were a single WHO surveillance estimate.

The **most credible emerging technology** is B7-H3–targeted ADC therapy for refractory osteosarcoma: the randomized ARTEMIS-011 trial demonstrated improved progression-free survival, but the cohort was small, follow-up short, and grade ≥3 treatment-related toxicity very common. The **hype check** is a new multimodal medical AI foundation model: external test sets are welcome, but strong benchmark accuracy and plausible visual explanations do not establish patient benefit or clinical readiness.

**Overall assessment: an important clinical week, not merely hypothesis-generating.** LEAP-Block and TroFuse-005 are especially strong because they evaluate clinical events or survival; IVF sequencing has immediate counselling implications. Other developments are promising but limited by regulatory access, short follow-up, surrogate endpoints, or incomplete external validation.

## Must Know

### 1. LEAP-Block: Left Bundle-Branch Area Pacing Reduces Heart-Failure Events Compared With Right-Ventricular Pacing

Citation: Li X, Wang H, Zhang W, et al. Left Bundle-Branch Area Pacing vs Right Ventricular Pacing for Atrioventricular Block and Preserved Left Ventricular Function: The LEAP-Block Randomized Clinical Trial.[^1]

Journal/source: *JAMA*, October 28, 2026

Publication type: Multicentre randomized clinical trial

One-line takeaway: In patients with atrioventricular block, preserved ejection fraction, and substantial anticipated ventricular pacing, physiologic conduction-system pacing reduced the 24-month composite of death, heart-failure events, or upgrade to biventricular pacing.

Evidence tier: High-quality randomized evidence using clinically relevant events; 482 participants.

Evidence maturity: Potentially practice-changing.

Practice impact: Possibly soon.

Canadian relevance:
Left bundle-branch area pacing and conventional pacemakers are already used in Canadian electrophysiology centres; this is not a new-drug approval question. Adoption will depend on electrophysiologist training, implantation time, device/lead practices, follow-up capability, and evolving Canadian pacing recommendations. No new national Canadian reimbursement determination specific to this trial was verified.

Importance score: 9/10

Key limitations:

* The adjusted composite rates were 2.4% with LBBAP versus 12.5% with right-ventricular pacing; the **unadjusted observed** proportions were 5.1% versus 13.3%. These are different analyses, not interchangeable event rates.
* The composite was driven principally by heart-failure events (3.4% versus 11.3%); all-cause mortality alone was low (1.7% versus 2.5%) and not shown to differ.
* Operator experience, lead performance, extraction challenges, and longer-term outcomes may limit generalizability.

Practical medical relevance:
This trial addresses a common decision in patients who need permanent pacing but do not yet have impaired systolic function. Preventing pacing-induced dyssynchrony could reduce later heart failure. In eligible patients, the results strengthen the case for discussing conduction-system pacing rather than automatically choosing a right-ventricular lead.

### 2. TroFuse-005: Sacituzumab Tirumotecan Improves Overall Survival in Previously Treated Endometrial Cancer

Citation: Lorusso D, Martin-Babau J, Backes F, et al. Sacituzumab tirumotecan versus treatment of physician's choice in previously treated endometrial cancer (TroFuse-005/ENGOT-en23/GOG-3095): a randomised, open-label, phase 3 trial.[^2]

Journal/source: *The Lancet*; ESMO Congress 2026, October 26

Publication type: Randomized phase III active-comparator clinical trial with overall-survival endpoint

One-line takeaway: In 776 patients with advanced or recurrent endometrial cancer after platinum and anti-PD-(L)1 therapy, sacituzumab tirumotecan improved median overall survival by 6.9 months versus doxorubicin or paclitaxel.

Evidence tier: High-quality randomized phase III survival evidence.

Evidence maturity: Potentially practice-changing, awaiting regulatory approval and implementation.

Practice impact: Possibly soon.

Canadian relevance:
Sacituzumab tirumotecan is **not the same molecule as sacituzumab govitecan**. Canadian authorization and public reimbursement for sacituzumab tirumotecan in endometrial cancer could not be verified. The FDA has accepted a priority-review application, but **priority review is not approval**. Canadian use remains trial- or access-dependent until a formal domestic decision.

Importance score: 9/10

Key limitations:

* Median follow-up was 14.0 months at the prespecified interim analysis; longer-term survival and safety remain relevant.
* Grade ≥3 treatment-related adverse events occurred in 54.4% versus 41.4%; anemia, neutropenia, stomatitis, and ocular toxicity require attention.
* The comparator was physician's-choice doxorubicin or paclitaxel, not every conceivable contemporary salvage regimen.

Practical medical relevance:
Median overall survival was **19.0 versus 12.1 months** (HR 0.68; 95% CI 0.55–0.84), and median progression-free survival was **7.2 versus 3.9 months** (HR 0.59). The absolute median survival difference is substantial in a post-immunotherapy setting with limited options. It is among the clearest near-term oncology practice-change candidates this week, pending access.

### 3. FIIX: First-Line IVF Produces More Early Live Births Than Four Cycles of Stimulated IUI

Citation: Prentice LR, Sadler LC, Anderson KL, et al. In Vitro Fertilization or Intrauterine Insemination in Unexplained Infertility.[^3]

Journal/source: *New England Journal of Medicine*, October 27, 2026

Publication type: Multicentre sequential randomized trial with noninferiority design

One-line takeaway: Initial stimulated IUI was not noninferior to IVF for live birth within the first treatment stage, although subsequent IVF access produced similar longer-term cumulative live-birth rates.

Evidence tier: High-quality randomized evidence with a patient-centred live-birth endpoint.

Evidence maturity: Ready to inform practice and counselling.

Practice impact: Now.

Canadian relevance:
Both IUI and IVF are available in Canada, but public funding and wait times differ markedly across provinces. The results should inform fertility counselling, especially when age, treatment urgency, out-of-pocket costs, and provincial IVF coverage alter the value of an earlier live birth. No new Health Canada product approval is involved.

Importance score: 9/10

Key limitations:

* Stage-1 analysis included 585 of 737 randomized couples, with pandemic-affected participants excluded according to protocol.
* After additional treatment, cumulative live-birth rates were **70% with IUI-first versus 71% with IVF-first**; the trial does not prove that IUI-first permanently reduces the chance of having a child.
* IVF involved more serious adverse events, largely related to oocyte retrieval or hyperstimulation, and cost/access may dominate the choice.

Practical medical relevance:
The stage-1 cumulative live-birth rate was **31% after up to four IUI cycles versus 55% after one complete IVF cycle**, an adjusted difference of −24 percentage points (95% CI −31 to −16). For patients who prioritize speed, first-line IVF has a clear advantage; for those prioritizing lower initial intervention intensity or cost, a staged strategy may remain reasonable.

## Important Developments

### 4. FDA Expands Talazoparib–Enzalutamide to HRR-Mutated Metastatic Hormone-Sensitive Prostate Cancer

Citation: US Food and Drug Administration. FDA approves talazoparib with enzalutamide for metastatic androgen pathway modulation-naïve or -sensitive prostate cancer with HRR gene mutations.[^4]

Journal/source: US FDA; TALAPRO-3, October 26, 2026

Publication type: Regulatory approval supported by randomized double-blind phase III trial

One-line takeaway: Talazoparib added to enzalutamide and androgen-deprivation therapy reduced radiographic progression or death in HRR-mutated metastatic androgen-pathway-sensitive prostate cancer.

Evidence tier: Randomized phase III evidence with a strong radiographic PFS signal; overall-survival evidence immature.

Evidence maturity: Ready for US practice; awaiting Canadian regulatory and access decisions.

Practice impact: Now in the United States; possibly soon in Canada.

Canadian relevance:
**Health Canada has authorized talazoparib plus enzalutamide for HRR-mutated metastatic castration-resistant prostate cancer**, a different disease state. For this **earlier metastatic hormone-sensitive indication**, the FDA states that Health Canada's Project Orbis review was still ongoing on October 26, 2026. Canadian approval and provincial reimbursement for the new indication were therefore **not established** by the US decision. Availability of the 0.5-mg formulation and local genetic-testing pathways also matter.

Importance score: 8/10

Key limitations:

* TALAPRO-3's major endpoint was **radiographic** PFS, not survival or quality of life; overall survival was immature.
* PARP-inhibitor toxicity includes cytopenias and warnings concerning myelodysplastic syndrome/acute myeloid leukemia.
* HRR alterations are heterogeneous; the aggregate result does not prove equal benefit for every included gene.

Practical medical relevance:
Among 599 participants, median rPFS was not reached with the combination versus **45.8 months** with enzalutamide plus placebo (HR 0.48; 95% CI 0.36–0.65). This reinforces the importance of tissue or circulating-tumour-DNA molecular profiling earlier in metastatic prostate cancer, while making it essential to distinguish US approval from Canadian access.

### 5. PHERGain Five-Year Follow-Up Supports PET-Guided Chemotherapy De-escalation—but Not Proven Equivalence

Citation: Pérez-García JM, Cortés J, Ruiz-Borrego M, et al. 5-year outcomes of chemotherapy de-escalation in HER2-positive early breast cancer and exploratory circulating tumor DNA analyses from the randomized phase 2 PHERGain trial.[^5]

Journal/source: *Nature Medicine*, October 29, 2026

Publication type: Five-year secondary analysis of randomized, **non-comparative** phase II strategy trial; exploratory ctDNA biomarker analysis

One-line takeaway: A PET- and pathology-adapted strategy allowed selected patients with early HER2-positive breast cancer to avoid chemotherapy with encouraging five-year outcomes, but the study was not designed to establish noninferiority to standard treatment.

Evidence tier: Prospective long-term phase II strategy evidence; insufficient for a definitive comparative treatment-de-escalation claim.

Evidence maturity: Awaiting confirmatory randomized implementation evidence.

Practice impact: Possibly soon in research settings; not routine standard care.

Canadian relevance:
PET, trastuzumab, pertuzumab, chemotherapy, and surgical pathology are available in Canadian breast programs, but a chemotherapy-free PET-adapted pathway is not an established national standard. Routine use would require prospective validation, consistent imaging and pathology workflows, and provincial guideline acceptance. The ctDNA component is exploratory and not a reimbursed decision rule established by this study.

Importance score: 8/10

Key limitations:

* The trial was explicitly **randomized but non-comparative**; its arms cannot establish that omission of chemotherapy is equally safe.
* The 86 patients who avoided chemotherapy and achieved pCR were a **post-randomization selected subgroup**; their excellent prognosis is not a causal estimate of treatment omission.
* Five-year iDFS was 89.5% in the adaptive arm versus 96.7% in the small standard-treatment arm, but no valid superiority/noninferiority inference follows from this descriptive comparison.

Practical medical relevance:
Among 267 patients in the adaptive arm, five-year invasive disease-free survival was **89.5%**; among 86 PET responders with pCR who received no chemotherapy, it was **92.4%**, with one distant recurrence. Baseline/persistent ctDNA was associated with worse outcomes, but treatment selection based on ctDNA remains unproven. The central opportunity is *safe de-escalation*, not an immediate claim that HER2-positive breast cancer no longer needs chemotherapy.

### 6. Single-Dose Intranasal Mebufotenin Shows Phase IIb Activity in Treatment-Resistant Depression

Citation: Feifel D, Seynaeve M, Cubała WJ, et al. Single Intranasal Administration of the Psychedelic Mebufotenin for Treatment-Resistant Depression: A Randomized Clinical Trial.[^6]

Journal/source: *JAMA*, October 26, 2026

Publication type: Randomized double-blind phase IIb low-dose-comparator trial

One-line takeaway: A single supervised intranasal dose of mebufotenin (5-MeO-DMT) improved depression scores at day 29 compared with a subperceptual dose in 193 adults with treatment-resistant depression.

Evidence tier: Moderately strong early randomized efficacy signal; small phase IIb trial with limited follow-up.

Evidence maturity: Early clinical evidence.

Practice impact: Possibly soon only after phase III and regulatory review.

Canadian relevance:
This formulation is investigational and **not a Health Canada–approved treatment** for depression. Psychedelic-assisted clinical research is subject to Canadian controlled-substance and clinical-trial rules; the trial does not authorize routine prescribing or unsupervised use. Cost, trained staff, monitoring, and psychiatric screening would shape any future rollout.

Importance score: 8/10

Key limitations:

* The comparator was a **0.3-mg low dose**, not a conventional antidepressant or active standard-of-care treatment; psychedelic unblinding remains possible.
* Follow-up extended only to day 57, leaving durability, relapse, and rare adverse effects incompletely characterized.
* Treatment was administered with psychological support and supervised observation; results do not generalize to home or recreational use.

Practical medical relevance:
The primary 12-mg comparison showed a day-29 MADRS mean difference of **−5.3 points** (95% CI −8.9 to −1.8). Day-29 remission was 18% at 12 mg versus 8% at the comparator dose; the 8-mg arm also showed a signal. The potentially shorter monitoring period is attractive, but the next question is whether benefits survive rigorous phase III testing and real-world safety surveillance.

### 7. A Self-Guided Internet Intervention Reduces Functional-Seizure Frequency in a Randomized Trial

Citation: Higson L, Chen Z, Rayner G, et al. Internet Intervention for Functional Seizures: A Randomized Clinical Trial.[^7]

Journal/source: *JAMA Neurology*, October 26, 2026

Publication type: Three-arm randomized clinical trial of a digital psychological intervention

One-line takeaway: A brief self-guided online program reduced patient-reported functional-seizure frequency and anxiety relative to standard care, including at 12 months.

Evidence tier: Randomized patient-reported clinical-outcome evidence, but modest sample size.

Evidence maturity: Awaiting replication and implementation.

Practice impact: Possibly soon.

Canadian relevance:
Functional neurological disorder and functional-seizure services are unevenly accessible across Canada. A validated self-guided intervention could improve access, especially outside specialist centres, but no Health Canada authorization, national recommendation, or reimbursement for this specific platform was verified. Clinical diagnosis and appropriate neurological/psychological follow-up remain necessary.

Importance score: 7/10

Key limitations:

* Only **107 patients** were randomized; 96 contributed to the full analysis set.
* The main outcome relied on seizure diaries, and blinding to a behavioural intervention was not possible.
* Therapist-guided delivery did not show a statistically significant seizure-frequency benefit over standard care; superiority of self-guidance over therapist guidance was **not** established.

Practical medical relevance:
The self-guided arm had a seizure-frequency rate ratio of **0.35** versus standard care (95% CI 0.18–0.69), with effects reported at 6 weeks, 6 months, and 12 months. The trial is noteworthy because it measures symptoms that matter to patients rather than app engagement or a digital benchmark. Replication, language adaptation, and equity-focused deployment are the next steps.

### 8. FDA Grants Accelerated Approval to the First Plasmid DNA Immunotherapy for Recurrent Respiratory Papillomatosis

Citation: US Food and Drug Administration. FDA Approves First Plasmid DNA-based Immunotherapy for Adults with Recurrent Respiratory Papillomatosis.[^8]

Journal/source: US FDA, October 30, 2026

Publication type: Accelerated regulatory approval based on an open-label multicentre study

One-line takeaway: The FDA approved Voceryq, an HPV-6/11-directed plasmid DNA immunotherapy delivered with electroporation, to reduce repeated papilloma-removal surgeries in adults with recurrent respiratory papillomatosis.

Evidence tier: Small single-arm clinical evidence using reduction in surgical interventions; confirmatory benefit not yet established.

Evidence maturity: Ready for selected US patients under accelerated approval; awaiting confirmation.

Practice impact: Now in the United States; possibly long-term elsewhere.

Canadian relevance:
**FDA-only approval was verified.** Health Canada authorization and Canadian reimbursement for Voceryq could not be verified as of November 1. Access would also require an electroporation device and experienced ENT/airway teams. It is a therapeutic DNA immunotherapy, not a replacement for preventive HPV vaccination.

Importance score: 7/10

Key limitations:

* The approval was supported by **32 adults** in an open-label study without a randomized control group.
* Among 26 patients with at least three baseline surgeries, **7 (27%)** needed no surgical intervention during the first year; regression to the mean and selection effects remain possible.
* The approval is **accelerated**, based on an intermediate clinical endpoint, and requires confirmatory evidence; electroporation-related adverse effects and logistics matter.

Practical medical relevance:
Recurrent respiratory papillomatosis can require repeated airway surgery over many years. A treatment directed at the underlying HPV-infected tissue, rather than repeated mechanical removal alone, is a meaningful proof of concept. Its practical value will depend on the proportion achieving durable reduction in procedures, airway symptoms, and treatment burden.

### 9. R-ALPS: Adding Anlotinib to Consolidation PD-L1 Therapy Increases Toxicity Without a Proven Incremental Benefit

Citation: Ji Y, Li Q, Chen L, et al. Benmelstobart With or Without Anlotinib After Chemoradiotherapy for Unresectable Non–Small Cell Lung Cancer: The R-ALPS Randomized Clinical Trial.[^9]

Journal/source: *JAMA Oncology*, October 29, 2026

Publication type: Multicentre double-blind randomized phase III clinical trial

One-line takeaway: Consolidation benmelstobart improved progression-free survival after chemoradiotherapy, but adding anlotinib did not significantly improve outcomes over benmelstobart alone and increased severe and fatal treatment-related toxicity.

Evidence tier: Randomized phase III comparative evidence; important negative incremental-treatment comparison.

Evidence maturity: Ready to inform treatment selection, but not a direct replacement for current Canadian standard care.

Practice impact: Possibly soon for research and guideline interpretation; not an immediate Canadian regimen change.

Canadian relevance:
The trial was conducted in China and **did not compare its regimens against Canadian standard consolidation durvalumab** after chemoradiotherapy. Benmelstobart/anlotinib availability and reimbursement for this indication in Canada could not be verified. The generalizable lesson is caution about intensifying post-CRT therapy without a demonstrated incremental benefit.

Importance score: 7/10

Key limitations:

* The pooled direct comparison yielded median PFS **17.4 versus 11.2 months**, but HR 0.82 (95% CI 0.63–1.08; P=0.12), so superiority of adding anlotinib was **not established**.
* Grade 3–5 treatment-related adverse events were **48.8% with the combination versus 29.4% with benmelstobart alone**; treatment-related deaths were 7.2% versus 1.4%.
* All trial centres were in China, and the control design does not answer whether benmelstobart is preferable to durvalumab.

Practical medical relevance:
In 553 patients, both active regimens beat placebo for progression-free survival, but the combination added substantial harm without a statistically established advantage over PD-L1 blockade alone. This is an important negative study in an era when multi-agent intensification can appear attractive on mechanistic grounds.

## Safety, Regulation, and Public Health

### 10. Canada's 2026 Public-Health Report Highlights a Widening Gap Between Longevity and Healthy Years

Citation: Public Health Agency of Canada; Chief Public Health Officer Joss Reimer. The State of Public Health in Canada 2026.[^10]

Journal/source: Public Health Agency of Canada, October 27–28, 2026

Publication type: Official national public-health surveillance synthesis and policy report

One-line takeaway: Canadian life expectancy has nearly recovered from the pandemic, but years in good health have not kept pace; youth mental health, sexually transmitted infections, toxic-drug harms, measles vulnerability, cancer trends, and wildfires remain priorities.

Evidence tier: Authoritative Canadian synthesis of surveillance, administrative, and survey data; not a causal intervention study.

Evidence maturity: Ready for public-health and health-system planning.

Practice impact: Now.

Canadian relevance:
This is **Canadian national guidance and surveillance context**, not a new drug approval or a binding clinical guideline. It is directly applicable to Canadian prevention, health-system planning, and risk communication, while provincial and local indicators should inform implementation.

Importance score: 9/10

Key limitations:

* The report is deliberately selective and not an exhaustive review of Canadian population health.
* Data series have different reporting lags; trend descriptions are not causal estimates of policy effects.
* National averages obscure important Indigenous, socioeconomic, geographic, and age-related inequities.

Practical medical relevance:
Life expectancy reached **82.1 years in 2024**, near the 2019 figure of 82.2, while health-adjusted longevity has not rebounded in the same way. Infectious syphilis rates increased more than fourfold between 2014 and 2023, and the recently ended measles outbreak involved more than 6,400 cases. The report argues for renewed investment in prevention, equity, and trusted public-health communication rather than equating recovery in life expectancy with recovery in population health.

### 11. Health Canada Recalls Specific Vitros HbA1c Slides Because Results May Be Falsely Elevated

Citation: Health Canada. Vitros Chemistry Products A1c Slides — health product recall, identification RA-82875.[^11]

Journal/source: Health Canada recalls and safety alerts, published October 29, 2026

Publication type: Medical-device performance recall (Type II)

One-line takeaway: Specified lots of Vitros HbA1c slides may yield positively biased HbA1c results, risking misclassification of glycemic control.

Evidence tier: Confirmed manufacturer/device-performance issue documented in an official Canadian recall.

Evidence maturity: Ready for immediate laboratory and clinical action.

Practice impact: Now.

Canadian relevance:
**This is an active Canadian recall** affecting specified lots. Laboratories using Vitros HbA1c slides should review lot numbers, manufacturer instructions, and quality-management procedures. Clinicians should consider repeat or alternate-method testing when an unexpected HbA1c conflicts with glucose measurements or clinical presentation.

Importance score: 8/10

Key limitations:

* The recall applies to **specified lots**, not all HbA1c assays or every Vitros slide.
* The public notice does not quantify the size of bias for each sample or the frequency of clinically important misclassification.
* As of October 15, the manufacturer had received 58 complaints and reported no adverse events; absence of reported harm does not eliminate clinical risk.

Practical medical relevance:
An inaccurately high HbA1c could lead to unnecessary medication intensification, hypoglycemia risk, or erroneous assessment of adherence. The operational response is targeted: verify assay lot exposure and reconcile discordant results with capillary/CGM glucose or a different laboratory method.

### 12. Bundibugyo Ebola Surpasses 10,000 Cases in the DRC as International Spread Continues

Citation: World Health Organization. Ebola disease caused by Bundibugyo virus — Democratic Republic of the Congo, Kenya and Nigeria; October 23 outbreak update. Associated October 30 DRC government figures reported by Reuters and Associated Press.[^12]

Journal/source: WHO Disease Outbreak News; Congolese government surveillance as reported by Reuters/AP

Publication type: Official outbreak surveillance with subsequent high-quality news reporting of updated national figures

One-line takeaway: By October 31, reported DRC totals exceeded **10,000 cases and 5,000 deaths**, with international importations and a continuing lack of approved Bundibugyo-specific vaccine or therapy.

Evidence tier: Official WHO outbreak documentation plus newer government figures reported by independent news agencies.

Evidence maturity: Ready for public-health action.

Practice impact: Now.

Canadian relevance:
There is no indication that routine Canadian clinical practice should change outside travel-risk assessment, preparedness, and infection-prevention protocols. Clinicians should rapidly isolate and contact local/provincial public health for compatible illness after relevant travel or exposure. WHO does not recommend blanket travel or trade restrictions; domestic response should follow PHAC and provincial procedures.

Importance score: 9/10

Key limitations:

* The **October 30–31 figures (10,351 cases, 5,026 deaths in AP reporting)** are newer government-based media figures, **not numbers from WHO's October 23 bulletin**.
* Underascertainment, conflict, and community deaths make surveillance and case-fatality estimates uncertain.
* No approved vaccine or specific antiviral exists for Bundibugyo virus; vaccine trials launched in October are investigational.

Practical medical relevance:
WHO's October 23 bulletin recorded **9,704 confirmed DRC cases and 4,687 deaths as of October 20**, with imported cases in Kenya and Nigeria. Later reporting documented further deterioration. The scale of this outbreak, high proportion of community deaths, and regional mobility make surveillance and infection prevention urgent even though the immediate risk to the Canadian public remains low.

## On the Horizon

### 13. Risvutatug Rezetecan Shows a Large PFS Signal in Refractory Osteosarcoma, With Very High Toxicity

Citation: Xie L, Yao W, Hua Y, et al. Efficacy and safety of risvutatug rezetecan versus gemcitabine plus docetaxel in relapsed/refractory advanced osteosarcoma: Primary results from the ARTEMIS-011 phase 3 trial.[^13]

Journal/source: *Annals of Oncology*; ESMO Congress 2026, October 26

Publication type: Randomized phase III active-comparator trial in a small rare-cancer population

One-line takeaway: B7-H3–targeted ADC risvutatug rezetecan extended median PFS to 5.5 months versus 1.4 months with gemcitabine–docetaxel in heavily pretreated osteosarcoma.

Evidence tier: Randomized phase III evidence for PFS, but limited by 122 participants, short follow-up, and immature survival.

Evidence maturity: Awaiting replication, longer follow-up, and regulatory implementation.

Practice impact: Possibly soon in China; longer-term elsewhere.

Canadian relevance:
Risvutatug rezetecan is **not Health Canada approved** for osteosarcoma, and Canadian reimbursement was not identified. The trial was conducted in China; international development is ongoing. The result may eventually matter to Canadian adolescent and young-adult sarcoma programs, but it does not yet justify routine off-trial use.

Importance score: 7/10

Key limitations:

* **Median follow-up was only 7.1 months**; overall-survival HR 0.51 had a wide 95% CI (0.21–1.23) and was not conclusive.
* **Grade ≥3 treatment-related adverse events occurred in 93% versus 83%**, and serious treatment-related events in 64% versus 29%.
* All 122 participants were enrolled in China; broader generalizability and long-term tolerability require evaluation.

Practical medical relevance:
Relapsed osteosarcoma has few effective systemic options. A PFS HR of **0.33** is notable, but the absolute median PFS gain was **4.1 months**, with substantial hematologic toxicity. It is an important therapeutic lead rather than a confirmed survival breakthrough.

### 14. MDT-BRIDGE Tests an Adaptive Surgery-or-Chemoradiotherapy Pathway After Neoadjuvant Chemoimmunotherapy in NSCLC

Citation: Reck M, Nadal E, Gay CM, et al. Neoadjuvant durvalumab plus chemotherapy in resectable or borderline resectable non-small-cell lung cancer: the phase 2 MDT-BRIDGE trial.[^14]

Journal/source: *Nature Medicine*, October 26, 2026

Publication type: Nonrandomized, single-arm phase II treatment-strategy study

One-line takeaway: Reassessment by a multidisciplinary team after neoadjuvant durvalumab–chemotherapy enabled most patients with initially resectable or borderline-resectable NSCLC to proceed to surgery or definitive chemoradiotherapy.

Evidence tier: Prospective feasibility evidence without a randomized comparator or definitive survival endpoint.

Evidence maturity: Early clinical evidence.

Practice impact: Long-term for the proposed adaptive pathway.

Canadian relevance:
Canadian thoracic oncology programs already use multidisciplinary review, perioperative chemoimmunotherapy in selected patients, and definitive chemoradiotherapy with consolidation durvalumab for unresectable stage III disease. The **specific response-adapted sequence** studied here is not established as a Canadian standard, and funding/eligibility for repeated immunotherapy across pathways requires local review.

Importance score: 7/10

Key limitations:

* The primary endpoint was **descriptive resection rate**, with no formal hypothesis testing or randomized standard-care comparator.
* Definitions of borderline resectability were made by local teams without uniform prespecified criteria.
* ctDNA clearance associations were exploratory and not validated for deciding whether to operate or irradiate.

Practical medical relevance:
Among **142 treated patients**, 74.6% underwent resection, 96.2% of those resected had R0 surgery, and **131 of 142 (92.3%)** ultimately received surgery or chemoradiotherapy. This suggests a feasible bridge between perioperative and definitive-CRT pathways, but it does not prove superiority over conventional multidisciplinary management.

## Hype Check

### 15. Explainable Multimodal Medical AI Is Externally Tested—but Not Clinically Validated for Patient Benefit

Citation: EVLF-FM study investigators. A clinical multimodal vision language foundation model with fine-grain explainability.[^15]

Journal/source: *Nature Medicine*, October 29, 2026

Publication type: Technical model-development and external-validation study

One-line takeaway: A multimodal medical AI model showed promising diagnostic and visual-grounding performance across imaging specialties, but no prospective clinical-outcome benefit was demonstrated.

Evidence tier: Substantial technical evaluation with independent test sets; no clinical effectiveness evidence.

Evidence maturity: Preclinical/conceptual for routine clinical deployment.

Practice impact: Unlikely to change practice soon.

Canadian relevance:
No Health Canada authorization or Canadian clinical reimbursement for this model was verified. Use in patient care would require prospective local validation, privacy and governance review, integration into clinical workflows, human-factors testing, and monitoring for performance drift and error.

Importance score: 6/10

Key limitations:

* Training/testing involved more than 1.3 million samples and external testing on 13,443 samples, but these are **datasets**, not a prospective patient-outcome trial.
* Reported internal diagnostic accuracy (0.895) and visual-grounding scores are not measures of diagnostic safety, reduced morbidity, or improved clinician decisions in real practice.
* Human ratings of explanation quality do not establish that generated explanations are faithful to model decision-making; hidden bias, automation bias, and failure modes remain unresolved.

Practical medical relevance:
The technical breadth—10 imaging modalities across six specialties—is noteworthy, as is evaluation beyond the development datasets. Yet **external benchmark validation is necessary but not sufficient**. A useful next trial would randomize real clinical workflows to AI-assisted versus usual care and measure missed diagnoses, unnecessary downstream testing, safety events, workload, and patient outcomes.

## Weekly Takeaways

**Most likely to change practice:** LEAP-Block. For patients with atrioventricular block and high anticipated pacing burden, left bundle-branch area pacing may prevent heart-failure events that would otherwise follow chronic right-ventricular pacing.

**Most important new therapeutic development:** Sacituzumab tirumotecan in previously treated endometrial cancer. TroFuse-005 demonstrates a randomized overall-survival benefit, although the drug remains investigational in Canada.

**Most important safety or public-health update:** The escalation of Bundibugyo Ebola past 10,000 reported DRC cases, with a high death toll and cross-border spread. For immediate Canadian clinical operations, the Vitros HbA1c recall is the most actionable specific safety alert.

**Most interesting emerging technology:** B7-H3–targeted ADC therapy in refractory osteosarcoma, because ARTEMIS-011 has a randomized comparator and a substantial disease-control signal. Its severe toxicity and immature survival are equally important.

**Most overhyped story:** The implication that a multimodal, explainable medical AI model is ready to improve patient care. The paper is technically interesting, but it measures model performance rather than clinical effectiveness.

**One development physicians may be underestimating:** FIIX. A 24-percentage-point early live-birth difference is consequential for patients making time-sensitive fertility decisions, even though eventual live-birth rates converge after further IVF.

**What patients may ask about this week:** Whether a new pacemaker lead can prevent heart failure; whether IVF should be the first fertility treatment; whether an experimental psychedelic nasal spray treats depression; whether a cancer drug approved in the United States is available in Canada; and whether new blood or imaging AI tests are clinically reliable.
