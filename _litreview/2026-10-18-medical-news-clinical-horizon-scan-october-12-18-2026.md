# Weekly Medical News and Clinical Horizon Scan: October 12–18, 2026

## Bottom Line This Week

The most clinically important development was the **ICRAKI randomized trial**, which compared initial intermittent hemodialysis with continuous renal-replacement therapy in critically ill adults with severe acute kidney injury. Ninety-day mortality was higher with intermittent hemodialysis, 63.1% versus 53.5%, and the trial was stopped early. The finding is potentially practice-changing, but early termination and the complexity of ICU dialysis selection mean it should be interpreted as a strong signal rather than a universal mandate that every eligible patient receive continuous therapy.

The most important hematologic development was a randomized frontline trial in **Philadelphia chromosome–positive acute lymphoblastic leukemia**. Ponatinib plus blinatumomab produced substantially better two-year event-free survival than imatinib plus intensive chemotherapy, approximately 86% versus 67%, while moving treatment toward a chemotherapy-minimized strategy. This is highly relevant to specialist practice and reinforces the broader oncology trend of replacing cytotoxic therapy with targeted and immune-based combinations when disease biology permits.

The most important infectious-disease result was **long-acting cabotegravir plus rilpivirine in adults with HIV and adherence challenges**. In the IMPALA trial, injectable therapy every eight weeks was noninferior to continued oral dolutegravir-based therapy among virally suppressed patients at high risk of oral-treatment failure. The practical implication is important: long-acting therapy may be useful not merely for convenience but as an adherence intervention for selected patients.

In gastroenterology, the oral IL-23 receptor antagonist **icotrokinra** showed clear phase II induction activity in ulcerative colitis. The efficacy signal is clinically credible, but this remains phase II evidence and should be viewed as a promising oral alternative to injectable biologics rather than an established standard.

Critical-care evidence was unusually rich. A randomized trial of **dobutamine added to norepinephrine during surgical source control for septic shock** reduced new or worsening postoperative AKI, while the large VASSPR pragmatic trial found no statistically significant mortality benefit from initiating vasopressin at a lower norepinephrine-equivalent threshold. SAVE-ICU likewise found no mortality advantage for inhaled isoflurane sedation over usual intravenous sedation.

The major public-health concern remained **Bundibugyo Ebola**, with transmission extending beyond the Democratic Republic of the Congo and three confirmed Kenyan cases by October 16. The most credible emerging technologies were once-weekly antiviral prophylaxis against dengue in a controlled human infection model, conversational AI that lets patients interact with seizure-monitoring systems, and direct intratumoral delivery of an EGFR-targeted antibody–toxin conjugate in recurrent glioma.

Overall, this was a **clinically important week**, led by randomized critical-care and hematology evidence. Several findings are actionable or close to actionable; the emerging technologies are scientifically compelling but remain too immature for routine use.

## Must Know

### 1. Intermittent Hemodialysis Is Associated With Higher 90-Day Mortality Than Continuous RRT in Severe ICU AKI

Citation: ICRAKI investigators. Intermittent or Continuous Renal-Replacement Therapy in Acute Kidney Injury.[^1]

Journal/source: New England Journal of Medicine

Publication type: Multicentre randomized clinical trial

One-line takeaway: Initial intermittent hemodialysis was associated with higher 90-day mortality than continuous renal-replacement therapy in critically ill adults with severe AKI.

Evidence tier: Randomized patient-centred mortality evidence.

Evidence maturity: Potentially practice-changing.

Practice impact: Possibly soon.

Canadian relevance:
Both intermittent hemodialysis and continuous renal-replacement therapy are standard ICU modalities in Canada. This trial could influence nephrology and critical-care practice, particularly for hemodynamically unstable patients, but local machine capacity, staffing, anticoagulation protocols, and patient selection remain important. Canadian guidelines should incorporate the full trial and its early-stop context before broad protocol changes.

Importance score: 10/10

Key limitations:

* The trial was stopped early, which can exaggerate estimated treatment effects.
* RRT modality is closely tied to hemodynamics, fluid goals, staffing, and local expertise.
* Findings may not apply uniformly to every ICU patient who could technically receive either modality.

Practical medical relevance:
Ninety-day mortality was 63.1% with intermittent hemodialysis versus 53.5% with continuous RRT. Because RRT modality has often been chosen on physiologic and logistical grounds rather than proven mortality differences, this result could materially alter ICU practice if confirmed and integrated into guidelines.

### 2. Ponatinib Plus Blinatumomab Outperforms Imatinib Plus Chemotherapy in Frontline Ph-Positive ALL

Citation: GIMEMA Investigators. Ponatinib–Blinatumomab or Imatinib–Chemotherapy for Adult Ph-Positive ALL.[^2]

Journal/source: New England Journal of Medicine

Publication type: Randomized frontline clinical trial

One-line takeaway: A chemotherapy-minimized ponatinib–blinatumomab strategy improved two-year event-free survival compared with imatinib plus conventional chemotherapy.

Evidence tier: High-quality randomized comparative evidence with a clinically meaningful event-free-survival endpoint.

Evidence maturity: Potentially practice-changing.

Practice impact: Possibly soon.

Canadian relevance:
Ponatinib and blinatumomab are available in Canada in selected leukemia settings, but frontline funding, sequencing, molecular monitoring, transplant strategy, and provincial formulary rules will determine implementation. The regimen is highly relevant to Canadian leukemia programs but should not be assumed to be universally funded or guideline-standard immediately.

Importance score: 9/10

Key limitations:

* Longer overall-survival follow-up remains important.
* Ponatinib carries arterial and venous vascular toxicity that requires careful patient selection and risk management.
* Treatment infrastructure for continuous blinatumomab infusion and molecular monitoring is specialized.

Practical medical relevance:
Two-year event-free survival was approximately 86% with ponatinib–blinatumomab versus 67% with imatinib–chemotherapy. The larger significance is therapeutic architecture: targeted therapy plus immunotherapy can potentially replace much of conventional chemotherapy in a molecularly defined leukemia.

### 3. Long-Acting Injectable HIV Therapy Works in Patients With Adherence Challenges

Citation: IMPALA Study Group. Long-Acting HIV Therapy in African Adults with Adherence Challenges.[^3]

Journal/source: New England Journal of Medicine

Publication type: Phase IIIb randomized open-label clinical trial

One-line takeaway: Cabotegravir plus rilpivirine every eight weeks was noninferior to daily dolutegravir-based oral therapy in virally suppressed adults with suboptimal adherence or care engagement.

Evidence tier: Randomized clinical evidence in a population specifically selected for adherence vulnerability.

Evidence maturity: Ready to inform practice.

Practice impact: Now where long-acting therapy is available and appropriate.

Canadian relevance:
Long-acting cabotegravir–rilpivirine is available in Canada for selected patients with suppressed HIV and no relevant resistance. The important Canadian implication is that long-acting treatment may be considered as an adherence-support strategy, although clinic access, injection capacity, insurance coverage, resistance testing, and missed-dose management remain practical constraints.

Importance score: 9/10

Key limitations:

* Participants were already virally suppressed at randomization.
* Baseline resistance testing was not performed in the trial, whereas resistance assessment remains important in real-world care.
* Long-acting treatment requires reliable clinic attendance even when daily pill adherence is difficult.

Practical medical relevance:
The trial directly addresses a group often underrepresented in long-acting antiretroviral studies: patients at risk of losing suppression because of oral adherence or care-engagement problems. This broadens the clinical rationale for injectable ART beyond convenience.

## Important Developments

### 4. Oral Icotrokinra Shows Phase II Efficacy in Ulcerative Colitis

Citation: Abreu MT, Siegmund B, Surace L, et al; ANTHEM-UC Study Group. Phase 2 Trial of Icotrokinra for Patients with Ulcerative Colitis.[^4]

Journal/source: New England Journal of Medicine

Publication type: Phase II randomized placebo-controlled trial

One-line takeaway: The oral IL-23 receptor antagonist icotrokinra produced a meaningful induction signal in moderate-to-severe ulcerative colitis.

Evidence tier: Randomized phase II efficacy evidence.

Evidence maturity: Early clinical evidence.

Practice impact: Possibly soon if phase III trials confirm benefit.

Canadian relevance:
Icotrokinra is investigational and is not an established Health Canada–approved therapy for ulcerative colitis. Its potential advantage is oral administration of an IL-23–pathway therapy, which could simplify treatment compared with infused or injected biologics.

Importance score: 8/10

Key limitations:

* This is phase II rather than definitive phase III evidence.
* Long-term maintenance efficacy and safety remain uncertain.
* Comparative effectiveness versus existing biologics and small molecules has not been established.

Practical medical relevance:
The drug targets the IL-23 pathway orally, addressing one of the main practical disadvantages of monoclonal-antibody treatment. If later trials reproduce induction and maintenance efficacy, it could become a major convenience and access advance in inflammatory bowel disease.

### 5. Dobutamine Added to Norepinephrine Reduces Postoperative AKI in Surgical Septic Shock

Citation: Chinese Perioperative Septic Shock Investigators for the Chinese Peri-Operative Organ Protection Group. Intraoperative Dobutamine Plus Norepinephrine to Prevent Postoperative Acute Kidney Injury in Septic Shock: A Randomized Clinical Trial.[^5]

Journal/source: JAMA

Publication type: Multicentre randomized open-label clinical trial

One-line takeaway: Adding intraoperative dobutamine to norepinephrine reduced new or worsening AKI within seven days after surgical source control for septic shock.

Evidence tier: Randomized clinical evidence with a prespecified kidney endpoint.

Evidence maturity: Potentially practice-changing within a narrow perioperative population.

Practice impact: Possibly soon.

Canadian relevance:
Dobutamine and norepinephrine are standard Canadian ICU and perioperative medications. The intervention is therefore immediately implementable from an access standpoint, but generalization should be limited to surgical septic-shock patients resembling the trial population.

Importance score: 8/10

Key limitations:

* The primary endpoint was AKI rather than mortality or long-term kidney function.
* The trial was open-label.
* Applicability outside surgical source-control cases is uncertain.

Practical medical relevance:
New or worsening AKI occurred in 22.8% with dobutamine plus norepinephrine versus 34.7% with norepinephrine alone. The finding suggests that hemodynamic strategy, not merely blood-pressure target, may affect perioperative organ injury in septic shock.

### 6. Earlier Vasopressin Initiation Does Not Significantly Reduce Septic-Shock Mortality

Citation: Peltan ID, Groat D, Jacobs JR, et al. Effect of Vasopressin Initiation Threshold on Septic Shock Mortality: The VASSPR Cluster Crossover Randomized Strategy Trial.[^6]

Journal/source: JAMA

Publication type: Pragmatic cluster-randomized crossover strategy trial

One-line takeaway: Starting vasopressin at a lower norepinephrine-equivalent threshold did not significantly reduce 28-day mortality.

Evidence tier: Large pragmatic randomized strategy evidence.

Evidence maturity: Ready to inform practice.

Practice impact: Now.

Canadian relevance:
Canadian ICUs commonly add vasopressin to norepinephrine, but the timing varies. VASSPR argues against assuming that earlier vasopressin initiation provides a mortality advantage solely because it reduces catecholamine exposure.

Importance score: 8/10

Key limitations:

* The intervention was a hospital-level strategy rather than fixed patient-level dosing.
* The mortality difference was small and statistically nonsignificant.
* Other outcomes and specific subgroups may still influence clinician choice.

Practical medical relevance:
Among 3,959 patients, 28-day mortality was 31.8% with the lower-threshold strategy versus 33.0% with the higher-threshold strategy. The result is useful precisely because it is negative: physiologic plausibility did not translate into a clear survival benefit.

### 7. Inhaled Isoflurane Sedation Does Not Improve Mortality in Acute Hypoxemic Respiratory Failure

Citation: Jerath A, Slessarev M, Martin C, et al. Isoflurane-Based Inhaled Sedation in Mechanically Ventilated Adults With Acute Hypoxemic Respiratory Failure: The SAVE-ICU Randomized Clinical Trial.[^7]

Journal/source: JAMA

Publication type: Pragmatic randomized clinical trial

One-line takeaway: Inhaled isoflurane sedation did not reduce hospital mortality compared with usual intravenous sedation.

Evidence tier: Randomized patient-centred clinical-outcome evidence.

Evidence maturity: Ready to inform practice.

Practice impact: Now.

Canadian relevance:
Inhaled ICU sedation requires specialized delivery and scavenging equipment that is not universally available in Canadian ICUs. The neutral mortality result argues against adopting the technology primarily for survival benefit.

Importance score: 7/10

Key limitations:

* The trial was open-label.
* Inhaled sedation has workflow and infrastructure requirements that vary substantially.
* Specific benefits such as wake-up characteristics or sedative-sparing effects may not be captured by mortality.

Practical medical relevance:
Hospital mortality was 42.5% with inhaled isoflurane versus 43.8% with usual intravenous sedation; ventilator-free and ICU-free days were also similar. The trial helps distinguish operational novelty from meaningful outcome improvement.

## Safety, Regulation, and Public Health

### 8. Bundibugyo Ebola Spreads to Kenya

Citation: World Health Organization Regional Office for Africa. WHO supports Kenya's Ebola response as third case is found early in quarantine.[^8]

Journal/source: World Health Organization

Publication type: Public-health outbreak update

One-line takeaway: Kenya confirmed three Bundibugyo Ebola cases, including one death, after importation from the DRC, documenting cross-border spread of the epidemic.

Evidence tier: Official outbreak surveillance.

Evidence maturity: Ready for public-health action.

Practice impact: Now.

Canadian relevance:
The direct risk to Canadians remains low, but cross-border spread increases the importance of travel history, prompt isolation, and public-health consultation in compatible illness. Canadian clinicians should follow current PHAC guidance rather than relying on US or WHO recommendations alone for domestic management.

Importance score: 9/10

Key limitations:

* Case totals are evolving rapidly.
* Transmission dynamics in Kenya may differ from the heavily affected DRC regions.
* Outbreak control depends on contact tracing, isolation, community trust, and health-system capacity.

Practical medical relevance:
By October 16, Kenya had confirmed three cases, two of whom were identified while already quarantined. The episode demonstrates both the risk of regional spread and the value of rapid contact tracing.

## On the Horizon

### 9. Once-Weekly Mosnodenvir Prevents Dengue Infection in a Human Challenge Model

Citation: Once-Weekly Mosnodenvir as Dengue Prophylaxis in a Human Infection Model.[^9]

Journal/source: New England Journal of Medicine

Publication type: Phase IIa randomized placebo-controlled human challenge study

One-line takeaway: Once-weekly mosnodenvir showed antiviral prophylactic activity against dengue in a controlled human infection model.

Evidence tier: Early controlled human efficacy evidence.

Evidence maturity: Early clinical evidence.

Practice impact: Long-term.

Canadian relevance:
Mosnodenvir is investigational and is not a Health Canada–approved dengue prophylactic. The main relevance is for future travel medicine and outbreak-response strategies if field trials confirm protection.

Importance score: 7/10

Key limitations:

* Cohorts were very small, with six participants per group in the reported cohort.
* A controlled human infection model does not replicate natural transmission or all circulating dengue serotypes.
* Field efficacy, resistance, safety with repeated use, and cost are unknown.

Practical medical relevance:
A safe once-weekly antiviral prophylactic could complement vaccines and vector control for travellers and outbreak settings. The current evidence is proof-of-concept, not a deployable prevention strategy.

### 10. Conversational AI Lets Patients Help Train Their Own Epilepsy Monitoring Systems

Citation: Goldblum Z, Shi H, Xu Z, et al. Human–artificial intelligence collaboration for comanaging epilepsy in real time.[^10]

Journal/source: Nature Medicine; NIH

Publication type: Proof-of-concept clinical technology study

One-line takeaway: A conversational interface allowed patients to query physiologic data and provide feedback that reduced seizure-monitoring false alarms by approximately 80%.

Evidence tier: Small prospective proof-of-concept human study.

Evidence maturity: Early clinical evidence.

Practice impact: Long-term.

Canadian relevance:
This is not an approved Canadian epilepsy-management platform. Its relevance is conceptual: patient-in-the-loop AI may be more clinically useful than passive black-box monitoring if it can safely personalize detection and reduce alarm burden.

Importance score: 7/10

Key limitations:

* The study was small and conducted in a highly monitored research setting.
* Reduction in false alarms is a technical performance outcome, not evidence of fewer seizures, injuries, hospitalizations, or deaths.
* Safety standards for patient-facing adaptive AI remain immature.

Practical medical relevance:
The system moved beyond one-way monitoring: patients could ask questions, receive individualized feedback, and correct the detector. That architecture may be important for chronic disease management, but prospective outcome trials are needed before deployment.

### 11. EGFR-Targeted Antibody–Toxin Conjugate Shows a Signal in Recurrent Glioma

Citation: Chandramohan V, Herndon JE II, Barnwal A, et al. EGFR-targeted antibody–toxin conjugate in recurrent glioma: a phase 1 trial.[^11]

Journal/source: Nature Medicine

Publication type: Phase I single-arm dose-escalation and expansion trial

One-line takeaway: Direct convection-enhanced delivery of D2C7-ATC into recurrent EGFR-expressing glioma produced encouraging survival and immune-activation signals.

Evidence tier: Early-phase single-arm evidence.

Evidence maturity: Early clinical evidence.

Practice impact: Long-term.

Canadian relevance:
D2C7-ATC is investigational and not a Health Canada–approved glioma therapy. Delivery requires specialized neurosurgical convection-enhanced infusion infrastructure.

Importance score: 7/10

Key limitations:

* Single-site, single-arm phase I design.
* Survival comparisons are vulnerable to selection bias.
* The procedure is technically specialized and not scalable without dedicated neurosurgical programs.

Practical medical relevance:
Among patients with IDH-wild-type recurrent glioblastoma, median overall survival was 7.9 months. Translational analyses suggested both direct toxin activity and immune activation. The more important signal is proof that intratumoral delivery can combine molecular targeting with local immune modulation.

### 12. Personalized Neoantigen Vaccination in Ovarian Cancer Is Feasible but Remains Early

Citation: Phase 1 trial of personalized neoantigen-targeting cancer vaccines in high-grade serous ovarian cancer.[^12]

Journal/source: Nature Medicine

Publication type: Phase I personalized-vaccine clinical trial

One-line takeaway: Personalized neoantigen vaccination combined with PD-1 blockade was feasible and generated tumor-specific immune responses in many patients.

Evidence tier: Early translational clinical evidence.

Evidence maturity: Early clinical evidence.

Practice impact: Long-term.

Canadian relevance:
No corresponding Health Canada–approved personalized neoantigen-vaccine strategy exists for ovarian cancer. Manufacturing complexity, turnaround time, cost, and patient selection remain substantial barriers.

Importance score: 6/10

Key limitations:

* Phase I study not designed to establish survival benefit.
* Personalized manufacturing limits scalability.
* Immunogenicity does not guarantee tumor regression or improved survival.

Practical medical relevance:
The study strengthens the biological case for individualized cancer vaccination and identifies mechanisms of resistance through cfDNA and antigen-presentation analysis. It remains a platform-development result rather than a treatment standard.

## Hype Check

### 13. “Conversational AI for Epilepsy” Is Promising, but Clinical Benefit Is Not Yet Proven

Citation: Lhatoo S, Vashin V. Conversational brain AI for adaptive epilepsy care.[^13]

Journal/source: Nature Medicine News & Views

Publication type: Expert commentary on an early proof-of-concept study

One-line takeaway: Patient-interactive seizure-detection AI is technically impressive, but reduced false alarms should not be confused with demonstrated improvement in seizure control or patient outcomes.

Evidence tier: Commentary based on a small prospective technology study.

Evidence maturity: Early clinical evidence.

Practice impact: Unlikely to change routine practice soon.

Canadian relevance:
No Canadian guideline recommends conversational AI as part of standard epilepsy monitoring. Regulatory, privacy, liability, cybersecurity, and clinical-validation requirements would all need to be addressed before routine use.

Importance score: 6/10

Key limitations:

* The underlying clinical study was small.
* The principal result was improved detector performance, not improved clinical outcomes.
* Adaptive patient-facing AI creates new safety, privacy, and responsibility questions.

Practical medical relevance:
The most useful interpretation is not that AI can “manage epilepsy,” but that human feedback may make monitoring systems more personalized and less noisy. The clinical value will depend on whether this translates into faster intervention, better quality of life, or fewer adverse events.

## Weekly Takeaways

**Most likely to change practice:** ICRAKI. A roughly 10-percentage-point difference in 90-day mortality between initial RRT strategies is difficult to ignore, although early stopping requires caution.

**Most important new therapeutic development:** Ponatinib plus blinatumomab in frontline Ph-positive ALL, because it substantially improves event-free survival while reducing reliance on conventional chemotherapy.

**Most important safety or public-health update:** Cross-border spread of Bundibugyo Ebola into Kenya.

**Most interesting emerging technology:** Patient-in-the-loop conversational AI for epilepsy monitoring, because it demonstrates a more adaptive model of medical-device AI than static prediction alone.

**Most overhyped story:** Conversational epilepsy AI as a near-term clinical management tool. Technical improvement is real; patient-outcome evidence is not.

**One development physicians may be underestimating:** The negative VASSPR and SAVE-ICU trials. High-quality negative critical-care studies are important because they prevent plausible physiologic strategies from becoming assumed standards without outcome benefit.

**What patients may ask about this week:** Whether dialysis type changes survival in the ICU, whether some leukemias can now be treated without conventional chemotherapy, whether HIV injections are suitable for people who struggle with daily pills, and whether AI can monitor seizures in real time.
