# Weekly Radiation Oncology Literature Surveillance Digest: July 27–August 2, 2026

**Coverage window searched:** July 27–August 2, 2026

**Scope searched:** PubMed, arXiv, medRxiv, bioRxiv, IJROBP, Radiotherapy & Oncology, Practical Radiation Oncology, JCO and ASCO publications, Lancet Oncology, Nature Medicine, Medical Physics, and Physics in Medicine & Biology.

**Note:** This is a best-effort literature surveillance digest, not a formal systematic review. Online-first publication dates were prioritized over issue-assignment dates. Papers were excluded when their first online publication clearly preceded the surveillance window, even if they appeared in an August issue.

---

## Bottom Line This Week

The most clinically meaningful paper is the **5-year update of the randomized phase III STELLAR trial**, comparing short-course radiotherapy followed by chemotherapy with conventional long-course chemoradiotherapy for locally advanced rectal cancer. The update strengthens the durability of short-course total-neoadjuvant-treatment evidence, although treatment selection still depends on anatomy, threatened margins, organ preservation goals, systemic-risk profile, toxicity, and local multidisciplinary practice.

The other major clinical paper is an **individual-patient-data meta-analysis of five randomized phase III trials** evaluating hormone therapy with postoperative radiotherapy for recurrent prostate cancer. Adverse pathological features were prognostic but did not clearly identify a subgroup with a different relative benefit from hormone therapy. This is clinically useful because it cautions against using pathology alone to decide whether to add hormonal therapy, particularly when the pre-radiotherapy PSA is low.

The most operationally relevant physics paper evaluated commercial deformable-image-registration algorithms for **mid-ventilation lung SABR contouring**. Starting contour propagation from the 20% respiratory phase performed better than from the 0% phase and approached interobserver variation. This is implementable workflow evidence, but it remains a small retrospective validation study rather than proof of improved local control or toxicity.

The infant-medulloblastoma and glomus-radiosurgery studies are clinically interesting but retrospective. The breast-reconstruction and head-and-neck oral-health papers provide useful counselling and survivorship information but are not radiation-prescription studies.

Nothing this week establishes a wholly new universal standard. The STELLAR update is the closest to practice-reinforcing evidence; most other findings refine patient selection, workflow, contouring, or counselling. Overall, this was a clinically meaningful week, driven more by mature outcome data than by AI or adaptive-radiotherapy innovation.

## Must Read

### 1. Five-Year Outcomes of Short-Course Radiotherapy Plus Chemotherapy Versus Long-Course Chemoradiotherapy for Locally Advanced Rectal Cancer

Citation: Tang Y, Zhou HT, Xu TZ, Li N, Jiang LM, et al. Five-Year Outcomes of Short-Term Radiotherapy Plus Chemotherapy Versus Long-Term Chemoradiotherapy for Locally Advanced Rectal Cancer: Updated Results of the STELLAR Trial. Journal of Clinical Oncology. Published online July 27, 2026.[^1]

Journal/source: Journal of Clinical Oncology  
Publication type: Peer-reviewed randomized phase III trial, 5-year update  
One-line takeaway: Five-year follow-up strengthens the evidence that short-course radiotherapy followed by chemotherapy is a durable alternative to conventional long-course chemoradiotherapy in appropriately selected locally advanced rectal cancer.

Evidence tier: High-level randomized phase III evidence with mature follow-up.

Importance score: 9/10

Key limitations:

- The comparison reflects specific STELLAR treatment schedules and may not represent every modern total-neoadjuvant-therapy regimen.
- Acute toxicity was higher with the short-course radiotherapy plus chemotherapy strategy in the original report.
- Organ preservation, MRI-defined risk features, surgical quality, and contemporary watch-and-wait pathways may affect applicability.
- Results from the participating centres and patient population may not generalize fully to all health systems.

Practical radiation oncology relevance:
This is the week’s strongest practice-facing paper. It supports short-course radiotherapy followed by chemotherapy as a legitimate total-neoadjuvant-treatment option rather than merely a resource-saving substitute. Selection should still be individualized according to circumferential resection margin, tumour height, sphincter preservation, organ-preservation intent, metastatic risk, toxicity tolerance, and multidisciplinary expertise.

### 2. Adverse Pathological Features and Hormone-Therapy Benefit With Postoperative Prostate Radiotherapy

Citation: Kishan AU, Sun Y, Parker CC, Sargos P, Sydes M, Chabaud S, Brihoum M, Supiot S, Latorzeff I, Guerif S, et al. Influence of Adverse Pathologic Features on Potential Benefit of Hormone Therapy Use with Postoperative Radiotherapy in Recurrent Prostate Cancer: An Individual Patient Data Meta-analysis. European Urology. Published online July 28, 2026.[^2]

Journal/source: European Urology  
Publication type: Peer-reviewed individual-patient-data meta-analysis of randomized phase III trials  
One-line takeaway: Adverse pathological features were prognostic after prostatectomy but did not clearly predict a different relative benefit from adding hormone therapy to postoperative radiotherapy.

Evidence tier: High-level individual-patient-data meta-analysis of five randomized phase III trials involving 4,781 patients and median follow-up of 9.1 years.

Importance score: 8.5/10

Key limitations:

- Trial populations, hormone-therapy agents, durations, radiation techniques, and treatment eras differed.
- The analysis focused on patients receiving postoperative radiotherapy, particularly those with pre-radiotherapy PSA values of 0.5 ng/mL or lower.
- Failure to identify a statistically significant interaction does not prove identical absolute benefit for every patient.
- Modern genomic classifiers and advanced molecular imaging were not uniformly incorporated.

Practical radiation oncology relevance:
This analysis argues against using adverse pathology alone as the deciding biomarker for hormonal therapy with salvage or postoperative radiotherapy. Decisions should integrate PSA level and kinetics, comorbidity, life expectancy, potential hormone-therapy toxicity, nodal risk, molecular imaging, pathological features, and the expected absolute rather than relative benefit.

## Worth Skimming

### 3. Deformable Image Registration for Mid-Ventilation Lung SABR Contouring

Citation: Yuen J, Shields-Dowton K, Poder J, Chin YS, Rezo A, Aren E, Hau E, Knox M, Lee J, Barber J, Howie A, Schmidt L, Chan DKE, Popovic G, Deshpande S, Holloway L. Facilitating the mid-ventilation SABR lung technique with deformable image registration: retrospective study into contour accuracy. Physica Medica. Published online July 27, 2026.[^3]

Journal/source: Physica Medica  
Publication type: Peer-reviewed retrospective technical validation study  
One-line takeaway: Commercial deformable-registration tools produced mid-ventilation lung SABR contours comparable with interobserver variation when propagation began from the 20% respiratory phase.

Evidence tier: Small retrospective contouring and motion-management validation study involving 27 lung SABR datasets.

Importance score: 7/10

Key limitations:

- The study included only 27 datasets.
- Contour similarity metrics do not establish improved tumour control, toxicity, or delivered-dose accuracy.
- Performance may vary by scanner, 4DCT quality, tumour location, motion amplitude, and software version.
- Commercial deformable registration still requires visual review and failure detection.

Practical radiation oncology relevance:
This is one of the more operationally useful papers this week. Centres using a mid-ventilation or mid-position lung SABR workflow may be able to reduce manual contouring burden and improve consistency. The 20% starting phase is a practical detail worth testing locally, but commissioning should include challenging lower-lobe lesions, irregular breathing, adjacent atelectasis, and explicit human review.

### 4. Radiotherapy Timing in Infants With Medulloblastoma by Molecular Subtype

Citation: Liu YF, Li XC, Zhang J, Niu JH, Du SX, Tian YJ. Real-world outcomes of radiotherapy timing in infants with medulloblastoma by molecular subtype: a single-center retrospective cohort study. Journal of Neuro-Oncology. Published July 30, 2026.[^4]

Journal/source: Journal of Neuro-Oncology  
Publication type: Peer-reviewed retrospective cohort study  
One-line takeaway: Radiotherapy appeared more important for non-SHH infant medulloblastoma than for SHH disease, in which selected patients had favourable outcomes with chemotherapy-led approaches.

Evidence tier: Single-centre retrospective cohort of 105 children younger than 3 years.

Importance score: 6.5/10

Key limitations:

- Treatment selection and timing were not randomized and are highly vulnerable to indication bias.
- Molecular-subgroup numbers were small, especially for Group 3 and Group 4 disease.
- Salvage-radiotherapy associations are susceptible to immortal-time and survivor biases.
- Treatment regimens, supportive care, staging, and molecular classification evolved over the 2013–2023 study period.

Practical radiation oncology relevance:
The paper reinforces that infant medulloblastoma is not a single disease and that radiotherapy avoidance or delay should be molecularly informed. It should not override cooperative-group protocols, but it supports careful subtype-specific discussion of the competing risks of disease failure and neurodevelopmental toxicity.

### 5. Postmastectomy Radiotherapy After Immediate Autologous Breast Reconstruction

Citation: Yong LY, Herieka M, Vyas R, Yiannoullou P, Sripadam R, Bennett SPH, Chagla L, Taghizadeh R. Postmastectomy Radiotherapy in Immediate Autologous Breast Reconstruction: A Patient-Reported and Clinical Outcomes Study. Plastic and Reconstructive Surgery. Published online July 28, 2026.[^5]

Journal/source: Plastic and Reconstructive Surgery  
Publication type: Peer-reviewed comparative clinical and patient-reported outcomes study  
One-line takeaway: Immediate autologous reconstruction followed by postmastectomy radiotherapy was associated with acceptable clinical, aesthetic, and patient-reported outcomes in a selected cohort.

Evidence tier: Observational comparative outcomes study.

Importance score: 6.5/10

Key limitations:

- Nonrandomized treatment selection and reconstructive expertise limit causal inference.
- Results from a specialized surgical and radiation team may not generalize broadly.
- Aesthetic and patient-reported outcomes can be influenced by baseline expectations, surgical revisions, and follow-up duration.
- The study does not resolve optimal sequencing for every patient who may require postmastectomy radiotherapy.

Practical radiation oncology relevance:
This paper is useful for multidisciplinary counselling. The need for postmastectomy radiotherapy should not automatically preclude immediate autologous reconstruction, but patients should understand the risks of fibrosis, volume change, fat necrosis, revision procedures, and cosmetic change. Planning quality, bolus decisions, internal mammary-node treatment, and close coordination with plastic surgery remain important.

## Save for Later

### 6. High-Resolution SPACE MRI and Target Delineation for Glomus Tumour Radiosurgery

Citation: Thimmarayappa A, Beniwal M, Saini J, Sadashiva N, Konar SK, Srinivas D, Arimappamagan A, Vazhayil V, Raj PA, Shashidhar A, Deora H, Birua GJS, Govindarajan N, Balakrishnan J, Natesan P. Dosimetric and spatial impact of high-resolution SPACE MRI on target delineation for glomus tumor radiosurgery: a retrospective cohort study. Journal of Neuro-Oncology. Published July 28, 2026.[^6]

Journal/source: Journal of Neuro-Oncology  
Publication type: Peer-reviewed retrospective imaging and dosimetric study  
One-line takeaway: Flow-suppressed high-resolution SPACE MRI generated larger glomus-tumour targets than conventional contrast-enhanced T1 imaging, producing potentially meaningful geographic and dosimetric differences.

Evidence tier: Retrospective imaging and planning study of 47 Gamma Knife patients.

Importance score: 6/10

Key limitations:

- No pathological gold standard established which MRI sequence defined the true tumour boundary.
- The estimated coverage loss was based partly on hypothetical application of existing plans to alternate contours.
- Corrective replanning may be affected by retrospective optimization bias.
- The disease is uncommon and the results may not generalize to other targets or MRI platforms.

Practical radiation oncology relevance:
For skull-base radiosurgery, millimetres matter. The study highlights how MRI sequence choice can change target definition at tumour-vessel interfaces and potentially alter coverage and organ-at-risk dose. It supports protocol standardization and multidisciplinary review rather than assuming all postcontrast T1 sequences are interchangeable.

### 7. Periodontal Deterioration and Salivary Dysfunction After Head and Neck Radiotherapy

Citation: Hassanein FEA, Ibrahim SS, William H, Almeslet AS, Aljudaibi SM, Abou-Bakr A. Longitudinal periodontal deterioration and salivary dysfunction following head and neck radiotherapy: a prospective multicenter cohort study. Odontology. Published online July 28, 2026.[^7]

Journal/source: Odontology  
Publication type: Peer-reviewed prospective multicentre cohort study  
One-line takeaway: Head and neck radiotherapy was associated with longitudinal worsening of salivary function and periodontal health, reinforcing the importance of preventive dental and survivorship care.

Evidence tier: Prospective multicentre observational cohort.

Importance score: 5.5/10

Key limitations:

- Observational data cannot separate all effects of radiation dose, systemic therapy, oral hygiene, smoking, diet, and baseline dental disease.
- Dental endpoints may vary with assessment methods and follow-up adherence.
- Detailed salivary-gland and oral-cavity dosimetry may not be available for every patient.
- The study does not test a specific preventive intervention.

Practical radiation oncology relevance:
The findings support pre-radiotherapy dental assessment, fluoride use, meticulous oral hygiene, smoking cessation, salivary symptom management, and structured long-term dental follow-up. For planners, they reinforce efforts to reduce dose to major salivary glands and oral structures when oncologically safe.
